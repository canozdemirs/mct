What is Finasteride used for?

Male pattern hair loss, commonly known as androgenetic alopecia, and benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) in males are both treated with finasteride. It is an FDA-approved drug. BPH isn’t cancerous. Nevertheless, BPH symptoms could also be a sign of prostate cancer or other more serious problems. Some of the other conditions that finasteride treats are,
  • Polycystic ovarian syndrome. Cysts in the ovaries are a symptom of the disorder polycystic ovarian syndrome.
  • Hirsutism, a disorder in which hair grows on body areas normally lacking hair
  • Male patterned baldness
However, the use of finasteride to prevent prostate cancer is not approved.

What is benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)?

The disease known as benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) causes your prostate to enlarge. Underneath your bladder and in front of your rectum is a gland called your prostate. It is around the size of a walnut and covers a portion of your urethra. Your urethra is a channel that allows you to expel sperm and urine from your body. The passage of urine and ejaculation through the urethra can be impeded if your prostate enlarges. For this reason, men with BPH typically have trouble urinating, a reduced flow of urination, hesitation when peeing, and a need to wake up in the middle of the night to urinate. Finasteride will lessen the severity of these symptoms and lower the likelihood that prostate surgery may be required.

Who are not suitable for Finasteride?

The use of this medication is not advised in the following situations. See your doctor if you experience any of the following symptoms:
  • Breast cancer (for a male patient)
  • Liver issues
  • Pregnancy
  • Breastfeeding a baby
  • High-grade prostate cancer

How does Finasteride function?

This drug works by reducing the amount of DHT, a bodily hormone that promotes prostate growth. The 5-alpha-reductase enzyme's activity is inhibited by finasteride. This enzyme converts testosterone to a different hormone, which in turn makes men's prostates enlarge or cause them to lose hair. As a result, the prostate will shrink and more hair will grow on the scalp. It will raise testosterone levels in the body. Only as long as the medication is used will finasteride's effects on the prostate and scalp persist. How Minoxidil is related to Finasteride?

In fact, several medications, including Minoxidil, more widely known as Rogaine®, can enhance the beneficial effects of finasteride. Although it is a topical treatment, minoxidil does not block DHT. Instead, it works by boosting blood flow to the bodily areas that require it the most. As a side note, Dihydrotestosterone (DHT) is a hormone that is crucial for the sexual development of those who are born as males (AMAB). In other words, DHT is specifically an androgen, a hormone that promotes the emergence of male traits. Considering that Minoxidil doesn't work in the same way as Finasteride, combining the two can improve how well each drug works to combat and perhaps even reverse the consequences of hair loss.

  • Wash your hands right away to get rid of any medication that may have gotten on them after using this medication.
