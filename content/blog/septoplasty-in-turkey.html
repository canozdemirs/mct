Septoplasty in Turkey Recovery Process

The barrier is a bone and cartilage wall that divides your nose into two separate nostrils. When the septum moves to one side of your nose, it creates a deviated septum. Some people are born with a detached nasal septum, but a nose injury can be the cause. Most people with a detached nasal septum have one nasal passage that is much narrower than the other. This can cause breathing problems. Frequent nosebleeds and facial pain can also be signs of a blocked nasal septum. Surgery is the only way to replace a crooked septum. The surgical procedure to repair a severed septum is septoplasty. The septum is straightened by septoplasty to allow for better airflow through the nose.

Recovery Process:

If no serious complications occur, septoplasty is usually performed on an outpatient basis. This means you can go home after anesthesia on the day of the procedure. To control bleeding, the nose is swollen, inflamed, and covered with cotton. One or two days after surgery, the shell can be removed. If necessary, your doctor will also give you pain medication. You will likely be asked by your doctor to stop taking aspirin, ibuprofen, and other blood-thinning medications. This is done after surgery to reduce the risk of bleeding problems. You can limit physical activity for a few weeks after surgery to reduce swelling and speed recovery. This includes most sports such as running, weightlifting, and contact sports. Such tasks can increase your blood pressure and cause heavy bleeding.

Tips for a faster recovery include:

  • Elevate your head at night to reduce swelling.
  • Do not blow your nose for at least three days after surgery.
  • Wear shirts with buttons in the front so you don't have to pull clothes over your head.
  • The sore on your nose will heal relatively quickly and your breathing will likely improve soon after the surgery. However, the whole recovery process can take a long time. It can take up to a year for the cartilage and other nasal tissues to fully adjust to their new shape.

Risks of Septoplasty

The possible risks of septoplasty include, but are not limited to:
  • Anesthesia risks
  • Change in shape of the nose
  • Decrease in sense of smell
  • Excessive bleeding
  • Infection
  • Persistence of previous symptoms despite surgery
  • Poor healing of incisions
  • Septal hematoma (collection of blood in nasal space)
  • Septal perforation (opening/hole in the septum)
  • Temporary numbing in upper gum or teeth
