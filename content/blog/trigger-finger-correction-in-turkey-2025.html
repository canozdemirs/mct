What is trigger finger, and what causes it?

Trigger finger is a condition that impacts the tendons in the fingers, causing them to catch or lock in a bent position. When trying to straighten the finger, a painful snap or click may occur. This happens due to inflammation or narrowing of the tendon sheath, which limits the tendon’s smooth movement. The condition is often seen in people who perform repetitive hand movements, such as typing or using tools, and can also be associated with underlying health issues like arthritis or diabetes.

How is trigger finger correction surgery performed, and is it painful?

Trigger finger correction surgery is a relatively simple and effective procedure aimed at relieving the pain and locking caused by this condition. Here's how it typically works:
  • Local Anesthesia: The procedure is usually performed under local anesthesia, which means you'll be awake but won't feel any pain during the surgery.
  • Incision: The surgeon makes a small incision at the base of the affected finger to access the tendon sheath.
  • Tendon Release: The tightened tendon sheath is carefully released, allowing the tendon to move freely and restore normal finger function.
  • Stitching: After the procedure, the incision is closed with dissolvable sutures.
  • Minimal Time: The entire procedure typically takes between 30 minutes and an hour.
As for pain, most patients report only mild discomfort after the procedure, which can be easily managed with over-the-counter pain relievers. The recovery time is relatively short, with many people returning to light activities within 1-2 weeks.

Are there non-surgical treatments available for trigger finger in Turkey?

Yes, there are non-surgical treatments available for trigger finger in Turkey, such as steroid injections, splinting, and physical therapy. These treatments can be effective for mild cases or when the condition is in its early stages. They help reduce inflammation, improve mobility, and alleviate pain temporarily. However, surgery is often recommended when non-surgical treatments do not provide long-lasting relief or if the condition is more severe.
