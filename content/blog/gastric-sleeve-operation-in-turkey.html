What is Obesity and How to Treat it?

Today, obesity has become an important health problem not only in developed countries but also in developing countries such as our country. The increase in obesity income levels, the establishment of a western lifestyle, the spread of fast-food type and standing snacks, and the high calorie and fatty diet have a great role in the increase in the obesity rate. Consuming excess calories with nutrition and not spending more calories triggers obesity. Today's people avoid physical activity, stay inactive for hours on the computer, television, and phone, while constantly eating high-calorie snacks. With the increase in transportation vehicles, people walk less, and they work less with the introduction of technological tools into our lives. The fact that the habit of doing sports is very low as a society has a great role in this increase.

What is Obesity and Morbid Obesity?

We can briefly define obesity as excessive fat accumulation in the body. WHO (World Health Organization) defines obesity as “abnormal, excessive accumulation of fat in the form and rate that will cause diseases in the body”.Morbid Obesity is defined as severe obesity that threatens life due to the fatal problems it causes. Those with a Body Mass Index (BMI) above 40 are considered morbidly obese. Body fat percentage is 25-30% in women and -20 in men. What are the dangers of obesity, what diseases does it cause?

Obesity causes disorders in all systems of the body. Experts say that obese people live 12-15 years less than their peers. It causes many diseases such as obesity, type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypertension, cardiovascular diseases, fat metabolism disorders, sleep apnea, reflux, sexual disorders, bone and joint problems, respiratory system disorders. Since the external appearance of obese patients deteriorates, their social life and mental health deteriorate, and many problems such as depression arise. Certain types of cancer, such as breast and colon cancers, are more common in obese patients.

How should obesity be treated?

In the treatment of obesity, there are 1. Diet 2. Exercise 3. Medication 4. Surgical treatment options. Diet and exercise play a major role in treatment. First of all, patients should change their lifestyles. Patients should receive good nutrition education and learn about the mistakes in this regard. Exercise must be a part of life. When all these are insufficient, drugs can be tried in certain groups of patients. However, most of them have serious side effects, and today there is still no drug that is very effective and has few side effects. Studies have shown that the rate of permanent weight loss with non-surgical methods in the treatment of morbid obesity is around 2%. Today, the permanent and effective treatment of morbid obesity is surgery. Obesity treatment is completely teamwork. A team consisting of endocrinology, general surgery, psychiatry, nutrition, and diet experts should evaluate the patient and determine the treatment method.

Those who have sleeve gastrectomy surgery have to meet the following conditions:

Obesity surgeries are not performed for aesthetic purposes, that is, to make the person look thinner.

How do you prepare for the operation?

If you qualify for sleeve gastrectomy, your healthcare team will give you instructions on how to prepare for surgery. You may need to have various lab tests and examinations before surgery.

Food and medicines

Before your surgery, give your doctor a list of all the medications, vitamins, minerals, and herbal or dietary supplements you take. You may have restrictions on eating, drinking, and what medications you can take. If you're taking blood-thinning medications, talk to your doctor before surgery. Because these drugs affect clotting and bleeding, your blood-thinning medication routine may need to be changed. If you have diabetes, talk to the doctor who administers your insulin or other diabetes medications for specific instructions on taking or adjusting them after surgery.

Other precautions

You will need to start a physical activity program. You will need to quit any tobacco use 12 weeks before surgery and a nicotine test may be done before surgery. You may also need to prepare for your post-operative recovery by planning ahead. For example, get help at home if you think you will need it. People who have sleeve gastrectomy are typically unemployed for four weeks.

Before the procedure

Before going to the operating room, patience will put on a gown and be asked a few questions by both doctors and nurses. Before your surgery starts in the operating room, you are given general anesthesia. An anesthetic is a drug that keeps you asleep and comfortable during surgery.

During the procedure

The specifics of your surgery depend on your personal situation and the practice of the hospital or doctor. Some sleeve gastrectomy surgeries are done with traditional large (open) incisions in the abdomen. However, sleeve gastrectomy surgery is typically performed laparoscopically; this involves inserting small instruments through multiple small incisions in the upper abdomen. The surgeon then creates a narrow sleeve by stapling the stomach vertically and removing the larger, curved portion of the stomach. The surgery usually takes one to two hours. After surgery, you wake up in a recovery room where medical staff watches you for any complications.

After the procedure

After sleeve gastrectomy, your diet starts with sugar-free, non-carbonated liquids for the first seven days, then progresses to pureed foods for three weeks, and finally to regular foods about four weeks after your surgery. You will need to take a multivitamin twice a day, a calcium supplement once a day, and an injection of vitamin B-12 once a month for life. In the first few months after weight loss surgery, you will have frequent medical checkups to monitor your health. You may need lab tests, blood tests, and various exams. In the first three to six months after sleeve gastrectomy, you may experience changes as your body responds to rapid weight loss, including:
  • body aches
  • feeling tired like you have the flu
  • feeling cold
  • dry skin
  • hair thinning and hair loss
  • mood changes

Results

Gastric sleeve surgery can provide long-term weight loss. The amount of weight you lose depends on the change in your lifestyle habits. It is possible to lose about 60 percent or more of your excess weight within two years. In addition to weight loss, sleeve gastrectomy surgery can improve or resolve conditions associated with being overweight:
  • Heart disease
  • Hypertension
  • high cholesterol
  • obstructive sleep apnea
  • type 2 diabetes
  • Stroke
  • Infertility
Gastric sleeve surgery can also improve your ability to perform daily routine activities and help improve your quality of life.

Does Hair Loss Occur After Gastric Sleeve Surgery?

Although rare, hair loss may occur after sleeve gastrectomy surgery. However, this is due to the inadequacy of substances that can nourish the hair. The most important reason is protein deficiency. In addition to protein deficiency; Deficiency of B vitamins, folic acid, iron, and zinc can also lead to hair loss. In case of hair loss, the patient's blood values ​​should be checked and the missing one should be replaced.

Do You Get Cold After Tube Stomach Surgery?

With the loss of weight, the decrease in the fat tissues under the skin can weaken the insulation system of the body. In this case, it can cause chills. Chills can be experienced not only in cold months but also in hot months. However, this is temporary. When the body adapts to its new weight after a certain period of time, the feeling of cold disappears.

Is Constipation Experienced After Sleeve Gastrectomy?

Whether constipation is experienced after sleeve gastrectomy surgery is related to nutrition. If the patient pays attention to his diet after the operation, constipation is not experienced.
