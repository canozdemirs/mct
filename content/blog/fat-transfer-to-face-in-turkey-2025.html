What is fat transfer to the face, and how does it work?

Fat transfer to the face, also known as fat grafting or fat injection, is a cosmetic procedure designed to restore volume, improve contour, and rejuvenate the face. Here's how it works: Step 1: Fat Harvesting
  • Liposuction: Fat is harvested from areas of the body with excess fat, such as the abdomen, thighs, or hips, using liposuction.
  • Preparation: The extracted fat is purified and processed to remove impurities, leaving only healthy fat cells for injection.
Step 2: Injection into the Face
  • Target Areas: The processed fat is carefully injected into specific areas of the face, such as the cheeks, under-eye hollows, temples, lips, or nasolabial folds (smile lines).
  • Technique: A fine cannula or needle is used for precise placement to achieve natural-looking results.
Benefits of Fat Transfer
  • Natural Material: Uses your body’s own fat, reducing the risk of allergic reactions.
  • Dual Benefit: Enhances the donor area (slimming effect) and improves facial volume.
  • Longevity: With proper technique, a significant portion of the transferred fat survives long-term, providing lasting results.
  • Rejuvenation: Fat contains stem cells that may improve skin quality over time.
Who is it for?
  • People with volume loss due to aging or weight loss.
  • Those looking for a natural alternative to synthetic fillers.
  • Individuals seeking facial contouring or enhancement.
Recovery and Results
  • Swelling and bruising are common in the first few weeks.
  • Results become more apparent as swelling subsides, usually within 1-3 months.
  • The body may reabsorb some fat (20-50%), but the remaining fat typically lasts for years.
This procedure is widely praised for its dual benefits and natural-looking outcomes.

What are the risks and potential complications of the procedure?

Fat transfer to the face is generally considered safe, especially when performed by an experienced surgeon. However, like any medical procedure, it carries some risks and potential complications. Here’s an overview: Common Risks and Side Effects: Swelling and Bruising
    • Swelling and bruising at both the donor site (where fat is harvested) and the injection site are normal and temporary.
Pain or Discomfort
    • Mild soreness or discomfort may occur but typically resolves within a few days.
Procedure-Specific Risks: Fat Absorption
    • A portion of the transferred fat (20-50%) may be reabsorbed by the body, potentially requiring additional treatments to achieve the desired results.
Asymmetry
    • Uneven fat distribution can occur if the injections are not placed symmetrically.
Over- or Under-Correction
    • Too much or too little fat can be transferred, leading to dissatisfaction with the results.
Infection: Infection at the Injection or Harvest Site
    • Though rare, infections can occur if sterility protocols are not followed.
Fat Necrosis: Lumps or Nodules
    • If the fat doesn’t integrate properly, it can form lumps or hard areas under the skin (fat necrosis).
Oil Cysts
    • Dead fat cells can form small cysts, which may require drainage.
Blood Vessel Complications: Fat Embolism
    • Fat accidentally injected into a blood vessel can block blood flow, leading to severe complications such as skin necrosis, blindness, or stroke. This is extremely rare but serious.
Skin or Nerve Damage: Skin Irregularities
    • Uneven texture or dimpling at the donor or injection site.
Nerve Injury
    • Temporary or permanent nerve damage can lead to numbness or altered sensation.
Allergic Reaction or Anesthesia Risks: Reaction to Local Anesthesia
    • Although uncommon, some individuals may have an allergic reaction to the anesthesia used during the procedure.
Long-Term Risks: Fat Migration
  • The transferred fat may shift from the injection site over time.
Uneven Volume Loss
  • As the body metabolizes the fat, uneven volume loss may occur, leading to asymmetry.

Are the results of fat transfer to the face permanent?

The results of facial fat transfer can be long-lasting, but not always completely permanent, as a certain percentage of fat naturally reabsorbs into the body over time. Within the first few months following the procedure, approximately 20–50% of the transferred fat may be absorbed. The remaining fat cells that successfully survive and integrate into the facial tissue can maintain results for several years, and in some cases, even longer.

What affects the longevity of results?

  • Surgeon expertise: A skilled surgeon using the correct technique increases fat survival.

  • Post-treatment care: Avoiding pressure on treated areas and following aftercare instructions improves fat retention.

  • Natural body changes: Weight gain, weight loss, and aging can influence the long-term appearance, as fat cells expand or shrink over time.

In some situations, a secondary fat transfer session may be recommended to enhance, refine, or prolong the outcome.
