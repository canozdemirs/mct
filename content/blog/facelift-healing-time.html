What is facelift?

Facelift Healing Time is an article that explains facelift operation. As we grow older, the elasticity of our skin and tissues diminishes naturally. This leads to sagging and wrinkles. Facelift, also known as rhytidectomy in the medical literature, is a surgical procedure that lifts and tightens these facial tissues. This procedure involves removing excess skin resulting from loss of elasticity, reducing wrinkles and tightening the skin. In this procedure, the focus is on the entire face and neck.

With aging, sagging, bags or lines around the eyes, prominent lines on the forehead, and sagging on the cheeks, chin and neck are observed in Turkey. This negative appearance, which occurs with applications performed to lift the face, can be eliminated and a more dynamic appearance can be achieved.

How is a facelift performed?

Facelift Healing Time is an article that explains the ways of facelift operation. “How is face lift surgery performed?” the question is also among those that are wondered. Before making the surgery plan, the person's youth photographs are examined and the aging pattern is observed to determine which parts of the face are deformed. During the surgery, small incisions of 1-2 millimeters are made on the skin using the endoscopic method, and the facial muscles, connective tissues and loosened tissues under the skin are treated. With the procedure performed on these tissues, sagging and loosened tissues are moved to where they should normally be and the deformation is eliminated. One of the curious questions about this procedure is whether it leaves any scars.    The size of the incisions made in the facelift application is quite small and they are made in a way that the hair remains inside. Therefore, there is no scar left after the procedure. Face lift surgery, which is the surgical procedure preferred by people who want to deal with the signs of aging, is performed in a sterile environment by expert plastic surgeons using local or general anesthesia.  

Facelift Surgery Methods

Facelift Healing Time is an article that explains the methods used in facelift operation.
  • Classic facelift (Traditional): An incision is made around the front of the ear, earlobe or ear fold, towards the back of the ear and the scalp, and the tissues under the skin are reached through this incision. With this procedure, skin loosening in the face, neck and chin area is controlled and tightened.
  • Standard facelift: Deformations such as looseness and sagging in the chin and neck area are eliminated through an incision in the classical facelift method. In this method, the lower area of ​​the face is targeted.
  • Endoscopic facelift: Smaller incisions are used in the endoscopic method. This method is based on the principle of performing operations with long thin instruments and a camera. At the end of this process, a smaller scar or no scar remains. The person's skin structure is the determining factor in this. However, in this method, excess skin cannot be removed in a single procedure, so it is generally used in upper face and forehead area lifting procedures.
  • Mid-facelift: It is used to correct the cheek and under-eye areas.
  • Periosteal facelift: In this method, the position of the deep tissues on the facial bones is corrected and the relationship between soft tissue and bone is restored.
  • Deep plane facelift: Aims to correct deformities in the lower eyelid, mid-face and cheek area.
  • Composite facelift: In this method, the skin, subcutaneous fat tissue and muscle are corrected as a whole.
  • SMAS facelift: This method, which is not effective in the mid-face area, compresses the tissues surrounding the deep structures of the face under the skin.
  • Full facelift: It is applied to rejuvenate the eyelids, middle and lower face, lips and neck area from the forehead.
  • Mini facelift: Only skin tissue is corrected with small incisions.
