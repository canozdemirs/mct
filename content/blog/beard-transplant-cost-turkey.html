What is Beard Transplantation and Who Is It Done?

If the beard does not grow in the areas where the beards grow or the density is low, the operation of transplanting hair follicles taken from other parts of the body is called beard transplantation. In the last ten years, great increases have been observed in the frequency of beard transplant operations. With the increase in the frequency of application, much more research and development activities have started on beard transplantation. Beards are considered an indispensable part of male aesthetics. As a result of the long beard fashion, today every man cares about the density of his beard. Even those who shave their beards daily can apply for beard transplant operations. People who need beard transplant operations:
  • They are those who do not grow any beards in their facial areas due to genetic or environmental factors.
  • They are those who do not grow a beard in a small area, although they have a beard throughout their face.
  • They are those who are not satisfied with its density even though they have beards all over their face.
  • They are those who want to change the beard density in one area of ​​their face.
  • Those who experience permanent beard loss due to wounds or burns.

How is Beard Transplantation Performed?

The methods used in the beard transplantation technique are the same as the techniques used in mustache transplantation and hair transplantation. With the increase in the demand for the application, modern techniques used in hair transplantation have been adapted to beard transplantation. Today, beard transplantation is done under two techniques, but the old FUT technique is not preferred. The main reason for this is that it has more disadvantages compared to the success it provides. FUE technique is used as a modern technique; In this way, serious successes are achieved. Regardless of the method, beard transplantation operations are carried out in certain stages:
  • The area where the hair follicles will be taken and the area to be transplanted are analyzed in detail. The amount of hair follicles to be planted is determined.
  • The area where the hair follicles will be taken is anesthetized, and the hair follicles are taken in varying shapes according to the method.
  • The area where the hair follicles will be planted is anesthetized, and the hair follicles are planted in varying shapes according to the method.
Regardless of the method used, the operations are performed under local anesthesia. In some patients, deep sleep is preferred. During the procedure, hair roots cannot be transferred from a foreign person. The hair follicles present in the patient's body are used. In general, hair follicles, that is, grafts, are taken from the nape area, but if the density in this area is low, hair follicles can be taken from other parts of the body. How much hair follicles are taken from the nape of the neck is also related to whether the patient plans a hair transplant after the operation. If the patient is planning to have a hair transplant operation after beard transplant surgery, as few hair follicles as possible are taken from the nape area.

What is the Recovery Process After Beard Transplantation?

In the first three days, crusting, redness, bruises, and edema occur. On the third day, the first wash is done and the crusts are cleaned. Shock shedding occurs between the second week and the fourth week. During shock shedding, redness, bruises and edema disappear completely. After two weeks, beards begin to grow slightly. Afterward, from the fourth month, the beards grow permanently. There is no disruption in the social life of the patient after the shock spill, all he has to do is wait for the beard to grow and protect the area from trauma.

What is Shock Shedding After Beard Transplantation?

The shedding of the strands on the hair follicles planted after hair, beard, and mustache transplantation is called shock shedding. It's a very natural yet frustrating process. The transplanted areas become completely bare within a week or two. This creates a psychological burden. Shock shedding is proof of a healthy planting. It is only the strands that fall out, the roots are not damaged. It is the usual result of the adaptation process. In some patients, shock loss does not occur. The patient, who is conditioned due to the information he reads and the information he receives from the doctor, may also get stressed because the shock spill does not occur when he expects it.
