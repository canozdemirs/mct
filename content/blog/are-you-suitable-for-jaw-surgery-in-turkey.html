What types of jaw surgery are available in Turkey?

Turkey is a leading destination for jaw surgery, offering world-class medical facilities, highly skilled surgeons, and cost-effective treatment options. Whether you're seeking surgery for functional improvements or aesthetic enhancements, there are several types of jaw procedures available, tailored to your specific needs.
  • Orthognathic Surgery (Corrective Jaw Surgery) This procedure corrects misaligned jaws to improve bite function, facial symmetry, and speech clarity. It’s commonly performed for individuals with overbites, underbites, crossbites, or open bites. Surgeons reposition the upper or lower jaw (or both) to align the bite properly, enhancing both function and appearance.
  • Mandibular (Lower Jaw) Surgery If you have a receding or overly prominent lower jaw, this surgery adjusts its position for better balance and proportion. It can correct a weak chin, severe underbites, or misaligned jaw structures, significantly improving chewing, breathing, and overall facial aesthetics.
  • Maxillary (Upper Jaw) Surgery For individuals with a small or protruding upper jaw, maxillary surgery repositions and reshapes the jawbone. It is commonly performed to treat facial asymmetry, excessive gum exposure when smiling (gummy smile), or difficulties with speech and chewing.
  • Genioplasty (Chin Surgery) While not always categorized as jaw surgery, genioplasty focuses on reshaping or repositioning the chin. It is often combined with jaw surgery to achieve a well-defined jawline and balanced facial proportions.

How do I know if I need orthognathic (jaw) surgery?

Orthognathic surgery, also known as corrective jaw surgery, is typically recommended for individuals experiencing functional, aesthetic, or health-related issues due to jaw misalignment. If you’re unsure whether you need this procedure, here are some key signs that indicate you may be a candidate: Difficulty with Biting, Chewing, or Swallowing If your teeth do not align properly when you bite down, it can make chewing inefficient or even painful. Misaligned jaws can lead to excessive pressure on certain teeth, causing wear and long-term dental problems. Speech Problems Jaw misalignment can impact speech clarity and pronunciation. If you experience difficulty enunciating certain words or have a lisp due to improper tongue positioning, jaw surgery may help correct the issue. Chronic Jaw Pain, TMJ Disorders, or Frequent Headaches Individuals with misaligned jaws often experience temporomandibular joint (TMJ) pain, which can cause:
  • Frequent headaches or migraines
  • Clicking or popping noises when moving the jaw
  • Difficulty fully opening or closing the mouth
Orthognathic surgery can help relieve chronic discomfort and improve jaw function. Facial Asymmetry or Imbalance If you notice an uneven facial appearance, such as a protruding or receding jaw, an open bite, or an asymmetrical smile, corrective jaw surgery may help create a more balanced and harmonious facial structure. Breathing Problems or Sleep Apnea A misaligned jaw can lead to airway obstruction, causing conditions like chronic mouth breathing, snoring, or sleep apnea. If you struggle with breathing difficulties, especially while sleeping, jaw surgery can open up the airway and significantly improve your breathing patterns. A Receding or Protruding Jaw A lower jaw that is too far forward (underbite) or too far back (overbite) can create both functional and aesthetic concerns. Corrective jaw surgery can reposition the jaw for a more natural and balanced profile. What’s the Next Step? How long is the recovery process after jaw surgery?

The recovery period for jaw surgery varies depending on the complexity of the procedure, but most patients experience a structured healing timeline:
  • First Week: Swelling and discomfort peak but are manageable with medication. A liquid or soft diet is required.
  • 2-4 Weeks: Major swelling reduces, and speech, eating, and jaw movement improve. Many patients can resume light activities.
  • 6-8 Weeks: The jawbone heals significantly, allowing for a gradual return to normal eating habits.
  • 3-6 Months: Full recovery is achieved, with minimal swelling and restored function.
