What is Septum?

The septum is the anatomical structure in the inner part of the nose that divides the nose into two compartments in the midline. The septum is partially bone and mostly cartilaginous. Under normal conditions, it is necessary to divide the nose into two equal parts so that equal breathing can be obtained from both nostrils. However, in almost 70-80% of people, there is a deviation in the septum, which is inherently structural or develops as a result of traumas over time, and these patients are diagnosed with 'septum deviation'. The operation performed to correct these curvatures is termed 'septoplasty' operation. Not every nasal curvature is operated. In cases where the nasal curvature is among symptoms like nasal congestion, difficulty in breathing through the nose, snoring, recurrent nosebleeds, frequent sinusitis infections, persistent nasal discharge or chronic headache, septoplasty surgery is required. So rhinoplasty, septoplasty and deviated septum are different complications. Apart from the septum, there are also anatomical structures called 'concha' inside the nose, which make it easier for us to breathe and are responsible for moisturizing the nose. Growth and swelling may occur in these structures, sometimes structurally and sometimes due to allergic causes. This makes it more difficult to breathe. More successful results are often obtained by intervening within the turbinates additionally to septoplasty operations.

What is Septum Deviation?

Due to the anatomical structure of the nose, the midline of the nose, called the nasal septum, should be positioned in a certain way so that the breathing function can be performed in a healthy and correct manner. However, due to genetic or subsequent traumas, the nasal septum, which is the cartilage and bone structure that forms the midline of the nose, may not be positioned correctly or may be curved. The situation where the bone and cartilage tissue formed by the nasal septum in a trapezoidal way in the midline of the nose makes it difficult for the person to breathe through the nose is called septum deviation. Septum deviation is a situation where breathing becomes difficult due to the curvature of the bone and cartilage structure in the nose, and in some cases, it becomes impossible to breathe when the nostrils are completely closed. Septum Deviation usually occurs due to physical traumas (hit, fall, etc.), and in some cases, it may occur during the process in the mother's womb or due to genetic reasons.

What Causes Deviation?

The most important cause of deviation formation is nasal trauma. These traumas include traumas in the womb, traumas during and after birth. However, often as a result of minor or major traumas in childhood, different growth points of the nasal septum are triggered, cartilage production increases and the septum cartilage and/or bone begins to bend as the growth continues. Sometimes a fracture line may occur in the septum cartilage. The severity, shape and direction of the deviation changes according to the severity and direction of the trauma. When Is Surgery Necessary?

The nose is the area at the center of the face that forms the focal point. The nose, which is called septumnasale in medical language, is like a tunnel divided in two. If these two tunnels are close to each other in width and length, we can talk about nasal health. These two parts, which are divided into two by the middle wall of the nose, are covered with a thin and membranous layer. There is cartilage in the posterior, anterior and upper parts of this layer, and a bone wall in the posterior lower parts. A healthy nose begins to expand from the posterior part of the narrowness in the anterior entrance hole. Exactly at this point, if there is any problem in the nasal tunnel from the entrance to the exit, problems related to breathing, which is our basic life function, occur. The most common cause of this condition, which causes the obstruction of air passage through the nasal tunnel, is the protrusion and bump formed by the middle wall of the nose. This protrusion and bump occur in a structure called the septum, which consists of cartilage and bone that divides the nasal cavity into two. The purpose of the septum is to divide the nasal cavity into two equal structures and to provide a systematic breathing process from these two air channels. Curvatures that occur for various reasons in this systematic and functional structure of the septum are called septum deviation. In the deviation of the septum, an obstruction occurs as a result of the shifting of the middle wall of the nose from the midline. Septum deviation is also known as nasal bone curvature. Septum deviation causes disruption of the balance of air spaces that perform systematic and alternating functions in the nose, thus causing various respiratory problems.

How is Septum Deviation Treated?

The curvature of the nasal septum, which should be thin and straight due to its normal structure, can make breathing difficult or impossible. If the curvature of the septum is curved or incorrectly positioned to cause nasal congestion, surgical intervention is performed. Surgery that corrects the curvature of the cartilage and bone tissue in the midline of the nose is called Septoplasty.

What Is The Difference Between Septoplasty and Rhinoplasty?

Rhinoplasty and septoplasty are both surgical nose operations. The most important difference between these two operations is that they are both performed for different purposes. While septoplasty is an operation related to air channels in the nose, rhinoplasty is a surgical operation to improve the appearance of the nose. These two operations can also be performed in a single session for the same person, and these operations are called septorhinoplasty. Sometimes these two nose surgical operations can overlap; For example, if a patient requesting rhinoplasty also has a structural defect such as a deviated septum in his nose, pre-correction for a predicted future septum problem can be made while performing the rhinoplasty. In such cases, two operation methods can be performed together. If you have concerns about which operation to have to solve the problem in your nose, you can consult a specialist surgeon; You can make a joint right decision by relying on your knowledge and experience. If you are not satisfied with the appearance of your nose and have difficulty breathing through the nose, if there is a deformation in the nasal structure below it, your nose can be corrected both functionally and aesthetically with septorhinoplasty, which is a joint operation by combining the elements of rhinoplasty and septoplasty operations. In septoplasty operations, the external structure of the nose is left untouched. However, if the patient is not satisfied with the aesthetic appearance of his nose, there is a suitable opportunity that the patient will want to evaluate in order to make aesthetic corrections together during the septoplasty. Aesthetic improvement will be achieved while nasal functions are corrected. Septoplasty and rhinoplasty are surgical operations that change the structure of the nose. Depending on the patient's discomfort and complaints, the nasal bones and cartilages are repositioned in both operations. Since the external structure of the nose is changed with rhinoplasty, it is a more comprehensive operation with a longer recovery period. Both operations are performed under general anesthesia. Since the goals of these two operations are different, the changes they cause in the nose are also different. Since the effects of rhinoplasty are external, it will be obvious to the eye, but since the internal structure of the nose is improved with septoplasty, the changes are not obvious from the outside.
