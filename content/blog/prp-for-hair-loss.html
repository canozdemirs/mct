What is PRP?

Injections of platelet-rich plasma (PRP) are becoming more and more popular for treating a range of ailments, from hair loss to sports injuries. Utilizing the patient's blood cells, the procedure quickens the healing process in a particular location.

Platelet-rich plasma is made up of two components: platelets, a particular kind of blood cell that is crucial to the body's ability to repair, and plasma, or the liquid part of blood. In addition to their well-known capacity to clot, platelets also carry growth factors that have the power to initiate cell division and promote tissue regeneration or healing in the treated area. Simply put blood with a higher-than-average platelet count is known as platelet-rich plasma.

 

 

A patient's blood sample is processed to create platelet-rich plasma, which is then injected into the desired location—such as a tendon or broken knee. The doctor may occasionally utilize ultrasonography to guide the injection. The goal is to speed up the healing process by increasing the concentration of particular proteins or hormones, known as growth factors, in a particular location.

Steps of PRP therapy

The steps below are an example of a typical approach to PRP injections for hair loss:

- A medical practitioner takes a blood sample from an arm vein.

- The blood sample is put into a centrifuge.

- The blood is spun in a centrifuge to separate its constituent parts.

- A medical practitioner uses a syringe to remove the platelets.

- The platelets are injected by a medical professional into certain scalp regions.

The entire procedure could take an hour or more, and multiple sessions might be required. A person can typically resume their normal activities without any restrictions following PRP treatment.

It is usually recommended to wait 24 hours after PRP therapy before washing your hair. This allows the PRP to completely penetrate the scalp and helps any mild adverse effects, such as redness or swelling, to go away. After the first 24 hours, you can resume your usual hair care routine.

Sadly, PRP treatment's effects on hair regeneration are temporary. Your natural tendency to hair loss cannot be altered, and if you do not receive maintenance-type treatment, your hair loss will return. The majority of individuals with male and female pattern hair loss who react well to PRP hair loss treatment need to have further treatment 12 to 24 months after the first session. Most minor side effects are temporary and disappear within a few days, such as temporary redness, swelling, or soreness at the injection site.
