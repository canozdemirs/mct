How to Grow Hair?

Human hair follicles produce hair both on the head and elsewhere. The hair begins to come out of the root or follicle. As it develops, it pushes up the hair on it. It stops in some body parts when it reaches a certain length. But the head is unique. The hairs on the top of the head (for men and women) and the beard (for men) continue to grow indefinitely, but this growth may slow down over time. Growing human hair is a natural biological process that occurs in hair follicles. Hair growth involves a cycle of phases: anagen (growth), catagen (transitional), and telogen (resting). For hair to grow, it needs several “components", including:
  • The protein cells that make up the hair itself
  • Blood, which feeds the root, brings oxygen and nutrients to it.
  • Oil to protect the hair from shine, softness, supple, and breakage.
  • Chemical stimulants called growth factors tell the hair to keep growing out of the follicle.
  • Productive follicles
While genetics play a significant role in determining hair growth patterns, there are several general tips to support healthy hair growth:
  • A balanced diet
  • Protein intake
  • Scalp health.
  • Avoiding excessive heat and styling
  • Protecting hair from the sun
  • Hydration
  • Avoiding tight hairstyles
  • Regular trims
  • Supplements
  • Reducing stress
But although these tips are applied, individual factors such as genetics, age, and underlying health conditions can also affect hair growth. At this point, if significant hair loss or changes in hair growth patterns are experienced, a hair transplant becomes a preferred solution.

1. Types of Stem Cells

There are several types of stem cells classified based on their origin, potential, and characteristics. Here are the main types: Embryonic Stem Cells (ESCs), Adult or Somatic Stem Cells (ASCs), Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells (iPSCs), Hematopoietic Stem Cells (HSCs), Neural Stem Cells (NSCs), Epithelial Stem Cells, Pericytes and Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs). These various types of stem cells have unique characteristics and potential applications in regenerative medicine, research, and therapeutic interventions for various diseases and conditions. However, especially Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) are commonly considered for hair regeneration due to their ability to differentiate into various cell types, including those found in hair follicles.

2. Mechanism of Action

The idea behind stem cell transplants for hair loss is that stem cells can stimulate hair follicles, promote hair growth, and potentially improve hair thickness. The regenerative properties of stem cells are thought to contribute to tissue repair and regeneration in the scalp.

3. Autologous Stem Cells

Autologous stem cells, specifically mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) derived from a person's tissues, have been explored for their potential use in hair transplants and hair restoration. This approach aims to harness the regenerative properties of stem cells to stimulate hair growth and improve the outcomes of traditional hair transplantation procedures. As with any medical intervention, individuals considering autologous stem cell-based treatments for hair transplants should consult with qualified healthcare professionals, such as dermatologists or hair restoration specialists, to discuss the potential risks, benefits, and personalized treatment plans based on current scientific knowledge and individual health considerations.In some cases, stem cells used for hair loss treatment are derived from the patient's own body, typically from adipose tissue (fat) or bone marrow. This is known as autologous stem cell therapy.

4. Procedure

The process involves extracting stem cells, processing them to concentrate and isolate viable cells, and then injecting the concentrated cells into the scalp, targeting areas affected by hair loss.
  • Extraction: For autologous stem cell therapy, the patient's stem cells are usually extracted from their own fat tissue or bone marrow through minimally invasive procedures.
  • Processing: The extracted cells are processed to isolate and concentrate the stem cells.
  • Injection/Transplantation: The concentrated stem cells are then injected into the scalp, targeting areas affected by hair loss.

While there have been studies and clinical trials exploring the use of stem cells for hair loss, the long-term efficacy and safety of stem cell transplants are still subjects of ongoing research. 6. Combination Therapies

Combination therapies in stem cell hair transplants involve the use of various treatment modalities, often in conjunction with stem cell applications, to enhance the overall effectiveness of the procedure. These combination approaches aim to address multiple aspects of hair restoration, including follicle stimulation, tissue regeneration, and improved graft survival. Here are some common combination therapies used in stem cell hair transplants:
  • Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP): RP is derived from the patient's blood and contains growth factors that may support tissue repair and stimulate hair follicle activity. When used alongside stem cells, it is believed to enhance the regenerative effects.
  • Adipose-Derived Stem Cells (ADSCs) with Fat Grafts: Adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs) are frequently used in hair restoration. Combining ADSCs with fat grafts (adipose tissue) is a method to provide a rich source of stem cells along with additional structural support for graft survival.
  • Topical Medications and Growth Factors: Topical medications, such as minoxidil, and growth factors may be used in conjunction with stem cell therapies to promote a conducive environment for hair growth and to maintain the health of existing hair follicles.
  • Microneedling: Microneedling involves the use of tiny needles to create micro-injuries in the scalp, promoting collagen production and potentially improving the absorption of stem cells. This technique is sometimes combined with stem cell treatments for enhanced efficacy.
  • Low-Level Laser Therapy (LLLT): LLLT, using devices like laser combs or helmets, is believed to stimulate hair follicles. When combined with stem cell therapies, it may contribute to improved hair growth outcomes.
  • Nutritional and Lifestyle Support: Comprehensive treatment plans may include nutritional and lifestyle recommendations to support overall hair health. This can include dietary supplements, stress management, and other factors that influence hair growth.
  • Follicular Unit Extraction (FUE) Techniques: Advanced FUE techniques, including robotic-assisted FUE, may be combined with stem cell therapies to optimize the precision and efficiency of graft harvesting and transplantation.
  • Cytokine-rich Plasma (CRP): Similar to PRP, CRP involves concentrating cytokines from the patient's blood, and it may be used in combination with stem cell treatments to enhance regenerative effects.
It's important to note that the efficacy and safety of these combination therapies are still areas of active research, and individual responses to treatments can vary. Before undergoing any hair transplant procedure with combination therapies, individuals should consult with qualified healthcare professionals, such as dermatologists or hair restoration specialists, to discuss personalized treatment plans and potential risks and benefits based on the latest scientific knowledge. Where are Stem Cells Obtained for Hair Transplant?

Stem cells for hair transplants are typically obtained from two main sources: Adipose Tissue (Fat): Many stem cells used in hair transplants are derived from the patient's adipose tissue, which is fat tissue. Adipose tissue is a rich source of mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), which have the potential to differentiate into various cell types, including those found in hair follicles. The process usually involves a minimally invasive liposuction procedure to collect a small amount of fat from the patient, and then the harvested fat is processed to isolate and concentrate the stem cells. These concentrated stem cells are then injected into the scalp, where hair loss has occurred. Bone Marrow: Stem cells can also be obtained from the bone marrow, although this method is less common for hair transplants. The bone marrow contains hematopoietic stem cells and mesenchymal stem cells. The bone marrow extraction process is typically more invasive and involves obtaining a sample from the patient's hip bone. The collected bone marrow is then processed to isolate the desired stem cells, which are later injected into the scalp. It's important to note that while there is ongoing research and interest in using stem cells for hair restoration, the use of stem cells in hair transplants is not yet a standardized or widely accepted practice. The field is still evolving, and the effectiveness and safety of stem cell-based treatments for hair loss are subjects of ongoing research. Before considering any stem cell-based hair transplant or other hair restoration procedures, individuals should consult with qualified dermatologists or hair restoration specialists. These healthcare professionals can provide personalized advice based on the latest research and guide individuals through the most appropriate treatment options for their specific situation.
