Is PRP approved by FDA?

FDA stands for food and drug administration. The FDA is closely involved in the clearance and approval of PRP treatments and the equipment that are used to make PRP since, theoretically, PRP is a "biologic" under FDA standards and because devices are needed to prepare PRP. The vast majority of PRP-making equipment is currently approved by the FDA. PRP procedures themselves are not subject to FDA approval before being utilized by practitioners because they employ a patient's blood and are not regarded as "drugs." It's critical to realize that while many procedures and treatments used often in clinical settings are not FDA-approved, they are nonetheless legal since they are designated as being "cleared" for usage. This is true for PRP treatments as well as the centrifuges that are employed to prepare PRP samples. Because of this, it can be claimed that the FDA has "cleared" rather than "approved" both the procedure and the equipment used to create PRP. PRP is a naturally occurring component of the human body, it is not a "drug." Perhaps this is the reason why some people in the treatment community and the general public have been perplexed about this matter. Practitioners are now permitted, both legally and morally, to employ PRP therapy for the benefit of their patients because platelet-rich plasma is not a medicine.
