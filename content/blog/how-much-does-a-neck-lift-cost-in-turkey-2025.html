What is Neck Lift?

A neck lift, also called cervicoplasty or platysmaplasty, is a cosmetic surgical procedure aimed at enhancing the neck and jawline by correcting sagging skin, excess fat, and muscle banding. It can be performed on its own or alongside a facelift to create a more youthful and defined look.

This surgery results in a smoother, firmer, and more contoured neck and jawline, boosting self-confidence and improving overall appearance, especially in photos or from different angles. The results are long-lasting, although natural aging will continue over time.

Excess Skin: As we age, the skin in the neck area loses elasticity, leading to sagging. A neck lift removes excess skin, tightening the area for a smoother appearance. Fat Deposits: Some people have excess fat under the chin or along the jawline, which can create a "double chin" effect. Liposuction is often used during a neck lift to remove excess fat and contour the area. Muscle Bands (Platysma Bands): Over time, the muscles in the neck (specifically the platysma muscles) can become more prominent and create vertical bands. A neck lift may involve tightening or repositioning these muscles to restore a smoother neck profile. Jawline Definition: A neck lift can help to define and refine the jawline by lifting and repositioning the skin and muscles around the neck and chin.  

The Procedure of Neck Lift

 
  • Pre-Surgery Preparation
Before the surgery, you’ll have a consultation with the surgeon to discuss your goals, review your medical history, and evaluate the structure of your neck and face. The surgeon will explain the procedure, potential risks, and expected results. You may also need to: Stop smoking (if applicable) for several weeks before surgery, as smoking can affect healing. Avoid blood-thinning medications (like aspirin) that can increase the risk of bleeding during surgery. Schedule pre-operative tests, such as blood work, if required by the surgeon.
  • The Day of Surgery:
On the day of the procedure, you'll be admitted to the hospital or surgical center, and the following steps will occur: Anesthesia: General anesthesia is commonly used during a neck lift, meaning you will be asleep throughout the entire procedure. In some cases, local anesthesia with sedation might be used if it's a less invasive procedure. Incisions: Traditional Neck Lift: The surgeon will make small incisions behind the ears, and sometimes under the chin, depending on the areas to be treated. These incisions are placed in areas that are discreet to minimize visible scarring. Liposuction (if needed): If excess fat is present, liposuction may be used to remove the fat, creating a smoother, more contoured neck and jawline. Muscle Tightening (Platysmaplasty): The platysma muscles (the muscles in the neck) may be tightened if they have become separated or loosened over time. The surgeon may stitch the muscles together to smooth the neck and reduce the appearance of vertical bands. Skin Tightening: Excess skin is carefully removed, and the remaining skin is pulled taut to create a smoother, more youthful neck profile. The skin is then repositioned, and the incisions are closed with sutures. Duration: A neck lift typically takes 2 to 4 hours, depending on the extent of the work being done. 3-6 Months: The full results of the neck lift will continue to develop as the skin and tissues settle. Final results can take 3 to 6 months to be fully visible, as any residual swelling completely disappears.
  • Post-Surgery Care:
Avoid vigorous activities or any actions that could strain the neck (like heavy lifting) for several weeks. Your surgeon will provide post-operative instructions on how to care for the incision sites, manage any discomfort, and promote healing. Follow-up visits will be scheduled to monitor your healing and ensure there are no complications.
  • Long-Term Results
Once fully healed, the results of a neck lift are long-lasting, offering a more youthful and rejuvenated neck and jawline. The effects can last for several years, although natural aging will continue to occur over time. Maintaining a healthy lifestyle and skin care routine can help prolong the results.

Recovery and Care

Immediate Post-Op Care: After surgery, you will be monitored as you wake up from anesthesia. You may experience some swelling, bruising, and mild discomfort, which is common and can be managed with prescribed pain medication. A compression garment may be worn around the neck to help reduce swelling and support the healing process. Recovery Timeline:
  • First 1-2 Days: Expect to rest and take it easy. Swelling and bruising will be most noticeable during this period.
  • First Week: Most patients experience mild to moderate swelling, and you may need to keep your head elevated to minimize it. The surgeon may remove stitches after 5-7 days.
  • 2-3 Weeks: You can usually return to light activities within 2 weeks, though strenuous exercise should be avoided for several weeks. Swelling should begin to subside during this time.

Risks and Complications

As with any surgery, there are potential risks and complications:  
  • Infection or poor wound healing
  • Numbness or altered sensation around the neck
  • Scarring (though well-hidden, scars may take time to fade)
  • Uneven results or asymmetry
  • Blood clots or bleeding
