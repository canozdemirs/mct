What Is Dental Implant?

A dental implant is an artificial tooth root that is placed in place of lost teeth. This is accomplished by placing screw-shaped implants made of biocompatible materials such as titanium or titanium alloys into the jawbone. These implants are placed under the gum and harden within a few months by fusing with the jawbone. This fusion process means that the implant is accepted by the jawbone and acts like a natural tooth root. The dental implant procedure consists of the following steps:
  • The dentist examines the area where the implant will be placed and evaluates the suitability for the dental implant.
  • Local anesthesia is applied to the area where the implant will be placed, and the gingiva is cut and the jawbone where the implant will be placed is opened.
  • The dental implant is placed in the opened area and the gum is sutured.
  • It takes several months for the implant to fuse with the jawbone and integrate with the bone.
  • An abutment is placed on the implant that integrates with the bone. The abutment is an adapter placed on the implant and is the part where artificial teeth will be attached.
  • Artificial teeth are placed on the abutment.
A dental implant provides a more natural look and feel than other dental prostheses and is a long-lasting solution. However, since the implant procedure is a surgical procedure, the patient should talk to the orthodontist in detail before the application.

What Are The Types Of Dental Implants?

Dental implants come in several types, which can be of different types and sizes. The types of implants may vary according to their intended use and other factors. The most common types of dental implants are:

Endosteal Implant

This is the most common type of implant and is placed under the gum. Screw-shaped implants made of materials such as titanium or titanium alloys are placed in the jawbone. After a few months, the implant fuses with the bone and artificial teeth are placed.

Subperiosteal Implant

This type of implant is located on the jawbone, but below the gum. Artificial teeth placed on the implant rest on the gum.

Transoseal Implant

This type of implant is placed where critical structures such as the mandible nerve are located. The implant is placed in the area between the jawbone and the lower jaw nerve.

Zygomatic Implant

This type of implant is used in cases where other implant types cannot be placed. The implant is placed in the zygomatic bone instead of the jawbone.

All-on-Four Implant

This is a type of implant used to replace all teeth. All teeth are replaced by using only four implants. While choosing between dental implant types, the dental polyclinics specialist dentist will determine the most suitable implant type according to the patient's needs, the condition of their teeth and oral structure.
