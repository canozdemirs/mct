What is Minoxidil?

Minoxidil is used to treat pattern hair loss. It is a vasodilator that can be purchased without a prescription in oral tablet form as well as over-the-counter in the form of a topical liquid or foam. Vasodilation, or the expansion of blood vessels as a result of the blood vessel's muscular walls relaxing, is a medical term. It is a technique to improve blood flow to body parts that are deficient in nutrients or oxygen, which are the hair follicles in this case.

What is Minoxidil used for?

In topical shampoos used to treat hair loss, the FDA-approved medication Minoxidil promotes blood and oxygen flow to the hair follicles. While hair loss is still in its early stages, minoxidil can aid in hair growth and stop further balding. To be more specific, minoxidil,
  • Prevents hair loss
  • Stimulates tiny blood vessels resulting in hair growth
  • Prolongs the anagen phase of the hair development cycle.
It works best on people under 40 who have recently lost hair. Minoxidil has little effect on receding hairlines. It does not treat baldness because the bulk of newly grown hair is gone within a few months after the treatment is stopped. What causes hair loss?

Your body's hair is a complex element. The anagen (growth) phase, catagen (pause) phase, and telogen (rest) phase, make up each follicle's unique life cycle, which lasts for several years. Your head has more than 100,000 hairs, so losing as little as 100 a day is not really concerning. Hair loss occurs when the anagen phase is disrupted, resulting in weaker or thinner hair, or if the hair follicle permanently remains in the telogen phase. This happens when hormones prevent the hair follicle from transitioning back to the anagen phase, causing it to remain in the telogen phase. Androgenetic alopecia, a genetic disorder, is the most typical cause of hair loss. As a result of the progressive hair follicle shrinkage brought on by this hereditary disorder, thicker, finer hair eventually develops. The follicle eventually quits making hair altogether. A hormonal sensitivity that is inherited from one or both biological parents leads to hereditary hair loss. Due to the pattern of hair loss at the head and/or temples, it is frequently referred to as male pattern baldness in men. Hair loss in women frequently has no discernible pattern. It may manifest as a widening part, spotty hair loss, or generalized thinning.

Who are the best candidates for Minoxidil?

The best candidates for Minoxidil are those who are just starting to lose their hair. Young men in their 20s make excellent candidates since minoxidil can delay the onset of more noticeable baldness. Before beginning treatment, it's crucial for anyone thinking about using minoxidil to speak with both their primary care physician and a hair loss expert. Minoxidil may be a viable option for women whose hair loss is caused by menopause or other hormonal changes.

How to prepare for starting using Minoxidil?

After you have understood how Minoxidil works, the second step is to prepare for taking the medication. There are some factors that may determine the effectiveness of the medication that you should be aware of. Take precautions according to the factors that are listed as follows,
  • Allergies. Inform your doctor and pharmacist as soon as possible if you have any allergies to any medications or minoxidil.
  • Other medications. You should let your doctor and pharmacist know if you're using guanethidine (Ismelin), any other prescription or over-the-counter medications for high blood pressure, vitamins, or any other medications.
  • Medical conditions.  If you currently have or have previously had a heart, kidney, liver, or scalp issue, let your doctor know.
  • Pregnancy. Inform your doctor if you are pregnant, plan to become pregnant, or are nursing a baby. If you become pregnant while using minoxidil, call your doctor right away. 
  • Protection. Use safety gear, sunglasses, and sunscreen. Minimize prolonged or excessive sun exposure. When you use minoxidil, your skin could become more sensitive to the sun.

Minoxidil can be applied topically as a solution or as a foam. If you notice any sunburn, redness, or abrasions on your scalp before using minoxidil, talk to your doctor.
