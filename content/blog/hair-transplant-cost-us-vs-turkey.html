What is hair loss?

Hair loss is also known as alopecia. It can affect the scalp or the entire body. Hair loss is mostly seen in adults. However, children can experience it too. Losing between 50 to 100 hairs a day is normal and unnoticeable because new hair will grow in the place of the lost hair. However, this is only sometimes the case. If a person is losing more hair than expected, it can be named as hair loss.

How to maintain bushy and shiny hair?

Any health issue in the body can affect the health of the hair. Factors such as hormones, seasonal changes, diet, skin diseases, and stress can lead to hair loss. In order to have bushy and shiny hair:
  • Try to stay away from stress because it weakens the hair follicles and leads to hair loss.
  • Wash your hair gently to avoid any damage. Wet your hands completely before applying shampoo. Avoid pouring shampoo directly on the head. Also, do not wash your hair with shampoo for a long time, and try to use warm water instead of hot water, which will frizz your hair.
  • Try to rinse the shampoo well enough. Avoid plastic combs. Instead, use combs made of natural materials so that your hair will not be electrified. Use a wide tooth comb if your hair is wet, and brush it after it is dried.
  • Do not make tight ponytails, as it will break the hair. In addition, try to avoid frequently using blow dryers and hair straighteners.
  • Stay away from excessive cold and heat. Change in temperature will damage your hair.

Dihydrotestosterone, or DHT, the primary hormone thought to be responsible for baldness, does not damage all of the hair on your head, which is why hair transplants can be successful. High levels of DHT, a testosterone byproduct linked to hair loss and associated with both male- and female-pattern baldness, have been found. A hair transplant surgeon can give you a thicker, fuller head of hair by shifting DHT-resistant hairs from the back and sides of your head to the front because DHT sensitivity signals the end for many hair follicles. What are the types of hair transplants?

There are two types of hair transplants:

FUE hair transplant

With the follicular unit extraction (FUE) method, each individual follicular unit is harvested from the scalp. The doctor will collect hair from the donor region with less obvious thinning of the area since the hair follicles are plucked in a random pattern. Incisions are microscopic. There are also two types of FUE hair transplants: manual FUE and robotic FUE. In manual FUE, a physician is extracting the individual hair follicles, whereas in robotic FUE, a physician is guiding a robotic device. The doctor decides how much hair will be harvested and sketches out the ideal donor region.

DHI hair transplant

In the more advanced hair transplant procedure known as DHI hair transplant (Direct Hair Implantation), individual hair follicles from the donor area of the body are carefully extracted before being put on the balding parts of the head.

FUT hair transplant

In the follicular unit transplantation (FUT), a long, thin piece of tissue is cut from the scalp's rear. Then, using stereo-microscopic dissection, individual follicular units are separated from the strip. Following the removal of the follicles, the wound is stitched up, leaving only a single, extremely small linear scar. Although both techniques have it own advantages, please see a doctor to make the right choice for you and your health.

Who are candidates for a hair transplant?

The robotic transplant is most suited for men with hair loss or balding on the top or front of their heads. It also depends on the density and stability of your hair in that region, as the hair is transplanted from a donor location at the rear of the head. Patients who would not be ideal candidates for FUT because they have soft scalps or little hair in the donor area can consider FUE. FUT is advised when a patient's main objective is to obtain the most possible fullness from hair restoration. FUT may offer better coverage and fullness than FUE for patients who have substantial balding in the crown region of the head.
