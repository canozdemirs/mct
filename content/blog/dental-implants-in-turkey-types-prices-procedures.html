What is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a surgical component that is placed into the jawbone beneath the gum line to support a dental prosthesis such as a crown, bridge, or denture. It serves as an artificial tooth root, providing a stable foundation for replacement teeth that look, feel, and function like natural teeth.

How Does a Dental Implant Look?

A dental implant itself is not typically visible once it is fully integrated into the jawbone and covered by the gum tissue. Here's how a dental implant looks in terms of its components and how it functions within the mouth:
  • Implant Fixture: This is the part of the dental implant that is surgically placed into the jawbone. It is usually a small, screw-shaped post made of titanium or titanium alloy. After placement, it is not visible because it is beneath the gum line.
  • Abutment: Once the implant fixture has fused with the jawbone (a process called osseointegration), an abutment is attached to the implant. The abutment protrudes above the gum line and acts as a connector to which the dental prosthesis is attached.
  • Prosthesis: The prosthesis is the visible part of the dental implant system. Depending on the number of missing teeth and the treatment plan, it could be a single crown for one missing tooth, a bridge for multiple adjacent missing teeth, or a denture for replacing a full arch of teeth.
  • Single Crown: When replacing a single tooth, the dental implant supports a crown that is custom-made to match the color, shape, and size of your natural teeth. It sits above the gum line and looks indistinguishable from surrounding natural teeth.
  • Bridge: If multiple adjacent teeth are missing, a dental implant can support a bridge. The bridge consists of artificial teeth (pontics) that are attached to crowns placed on the adjacent dental implants. This restores the appearance and function of multiple missing teeth in a row.
  • Denture: For replacing a full arch of missing teeth, several dental implants can be strategically placed in the jawbone to support a denture. The denture attaches securely to the implants via attachments or clasps, providing stability and preventing slippage.
Overall, while the dental implant itself (the fixture and abutment) is not visible, the prosthesis that attaches to it is designed to blend seamlessly with your natural teeth. Modern dental technology and materials ensure that dental implants not only restore function but also enhance the aesthetic appearance of your smile.  

Types of Dental Implants in Turkey

In Turkey, as in many other countries, various types of dental implants are available to cater to different patient needs and dental conditions. Here are some common types of dental implants you can find in Turkey: Endosteal Implants: Endosteal implants are the most common type used worldwide. They resemble small screws or cylinders and provide a stable foundation for artificial teeth. Subperiosteal Implants: Subperiosteal implants are less common and are placed under the gum but on, or above, the jawbone. They are used when the jawbone has receded and cannot support endosteal implants. Subperiosteal implants consist of a metal framework that protrudes through the gum to hold the artificial tooth or teeth. Zygomatic Implants: Zygomatic implants are used in cases where the patient has insufficient bone in the upper jaw (maxilla) for traditional implants. These implants are anchored into the cheekbone rather than the maxillary bone. All-on-4 / All-on-6 Implants: These are specific techniques for full-arch rehabilitation where a fixed dental prosthesis (denture) is supported by only 4 or 6 implants per arch. This approach reduces the number of implants required and can often avoid the need for bone grafting. Mini Implants: Mini implants are smaller in diameter compared to traditional implants. They are often used in cases where there is minimal bone density or when there is limited space, such as for supporting removable dentures. Immediate Load Implants: Also known as same-day implants or teeth-in-a-day, immediate load implants allow for the placement of a temporary crown or denture on the same day as the implant surgery. This technique can be advantageous for patients who desire quicker restoration of their smile. Ceramic Implants: Ceramic or zirconia implants are an alternative to traditional titanium implants. They are known for their aesthetic appeal as they can blend more naturally with the surrounding teeth and gums. They are also hypoallergenic, making them suitable for patients with metal allergies. When considering which type of dental implant is best for you, it is essential to consult with a qualified dentist or implant specialist. They will evaluate your oral health, jawbone density, and specific needs to recommend the most suitable implant type and treatment plan. In Turkey, many dental clinics offer a range of implant options, and they can provide detailed information about the benefits and costs associated with each type.  

Dental Implants Prices in Turkey

The cost of dental implants in Turkey can vary depending on several factors such as the type of implant, the number of implants needed, the location and reputation of the clinic, and the expertise of the dentist. However, generally speaking, dental implants in Turkey are known to be more affordable compared to many Western countries. Here are some approximate price ranges you might expect: Single Dental Implant: Prices typically range from €300 per implant. This cost usually includes the implant fixture, abutment, and crown. Multiple Dental Implants (for several missing teeth): If you need multiple implants, the total cost will depend on the number of implants required. Clinics may offer package deals or discounts for multiple implants. Full Arch Implants (for replacing all teeth in one arch): For full arch implants supported by a fixed denture or bridge, costs can vary significantly. Prices might range from €8,000 to €15,000 or more, depending on the complexity and materials used. It is important to note that these prices are approximate and can vary based on individual factors such as the specific clinic's pricing structure, the materials used (e.g., titanium implants vs. zirconia implants), additional procedures needed (like bone grafting or sinus lifts), and any associated fees for consultations, X-rays, or aftercare. Factors contributing to the affordability of dental implants in Turkey include lower labor costs, competitive market pricing, and favorable exchange rates for international patients. Dental Implant Procedure in Turkey

The dental implant procedure is typically performed in several stages and requires coordination between the patient, dentist, and possibly other specialists like oral surgeons or periodontists. Here's a general outline of how a dental implant procedure is done: Initial Consultation and Treatment Planning Assessment: The dentist will examine your teeth, gums, and overall oral health. Treatment Plan: Based on the assessment, the dentist will discuss treatment options, including the type of implant suitable for your case, the number of implants needed, and any additional procedures required (such as bone grafting if there's insufficient bone). Implant Placement Surgery Anesthesia: Local anesthesia is usually administered to numb the surgical area. Incision: The dentist makes an incision in the gum tissue to expose the jawbone where the implant will be placed. Drilling: Using precise surgical instruments, the dentist drills a hole into the bone according to the predetermined depth and width required for the implant. Implant Placement: The dental implant, usually a titanium screw or cylinder, is carefully placed into the prepared socket in the jawbone. Sometimes, a healing cap or cover screw may be placed over the implant to protect it during the healing phase. Healing and Osseointegration Healing Period: Over the next several weeks to months, the implant undergoes a process called osseointegration, where it integrates with the surrounding bone tissue. Abutment Placement (Second Surgery, if needed) Exposure: Once osseointegration is complete, a minor surgery may be needed to expose the implant if it was initially covered by gum tissue or a healing cap. Abutment Attachment: An abutment (connector piece) is placed on top of the implant. This abutment will eventually support the dental prosthesis (crown, bridge, or denture). Prosthesis Fabrication and Placement Impressions: After the gums have healed around the abutment, impressions of your teeth and bite are taken to create the final prosthesis. Prosthesis Placement: The dental prosthesis (crown, bridge, or denture) is then securely attached to the abutment. The prosthesis is custom-made to match of your natural teeth. Follow-up Care and Maintenance Check-ups: Regular follow-up visits are scheduled to monitor the implant's stability and the health of surrounding tissues. Oral Care: Proper oral hygiene practices, including brushing, flossing, and regular dental cleanings, are essential to maintain the health of the implant and surrounding teeth. Considerations:
  • Bone Grafting: If there is insufficient bone to support the implant, bone grafting procedures may be performed beforehand to augment the bone volume.
  • Sinus Lift: For implants in the upper jaw, a sinus lift may be necessary to increase bone height in the posterior maxilla.
Throughout the entire process, your dentist will provide detailed instructions on post-operative care and what to expect during each stage of healing. The success rate of dental implants is high, with proper care and maintenance ensuring long-term functionality and aesthetics.

What is the Criteria for Getting a Dental Implant?

Several criteria determine whether a person is a suitable candidate for dental implants. These criteria help ensure the success and longevity of the implant treatment. Here are the main factors considered:
  • Sufficient Bone Density: Adequate bone density and volume in the jawbone are essential to support the dental implant. If there is significant bone loss or insufficient bone structure due to factors like periodontal disease, trauma, or long-term tooth loss, bone grafting procedures may be necessary to augment the bone before implant placement.
  • Good Oral Health: The patient should have overall good oral health, including healthy gums free from periodontal disease. Any existing dental issues, such as decay or gum disease, should be treated before proceeding with implant surgery.
  • General Health and Well-being: The patient's general health is also a crucial factor. Conditions such as uncontrolled diabetes, autoimmune disorders, or significant immune deficiencies may affect healing after surgery and increase the risk of implant failure. A thorough medical history review is necessary to assess overall health status.
  • Commitment to Oral Hygiene: Patients should be committed to regular brushing, flossing, and attending dental check-ups to ensure the health of the implants and surrounding teeth and gums.
  • Non-Smoker or Willingness to Quit: Smoking or tobacco use can impair healing and increase the risk of implant failure. Ideally, patients should be non-smokers or willing to quit smoking before undergoing implant surgery to improve outcomes.
  • Age Considerations: While age alone is not a determining factor, patients should generally have completed jawbone growth, typically around late adolescence or early adulthood, before considering dental implants.
  • Treatment Expectations: Patients should have realistic expectations about the outcomes of dental implant treatment. Understanding the procedure, recovery process, and potential risks involved is crucial for informed decision-making.
  • Consultation and Evaluation: A comprehensive evaluation by a qualified dentist or oral surgeon is necessary to assess whether dental implants are suitable for the individual patient's needs and circumstances. This evaluation may include dental exams, imaging (X-rays, CT scans), and discussions about treatment goals and options.
Ultimately, the decision to proceed with dental implants should be made in consultation with a dental professional who can assess your specific oral health, medical history, and treatment goals. They will help determine if you meet the criteria for dental implants and create a personalized treatment plan to achieve optimal results.

What are the Pre-existing Conditions to Consider Before Getting a Dental Implant?

Before undergoing a dental implant procedure, it's important to consider pre-existing medical and dental conditions that may affect the success and outcome of the treatment. Here are some key conditions to discuss with your dentist or oral surgeon:
  • Uncontrolled Diabetes: Poorly controlled diabetes can impair healing after surgery and increase the risk of complications, including implant failure. It's essential for diabetic patients to have their condition well-managed and stable before proceeding with dental implants.
  • Autoimmune Disorders: Conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, or other autoimmune diseases may affect healing and increase the risk of implant rejection. Patients with autoimmune disorders should be evaluated on a case-by-case basis to determine the suitability of dental implants.
  • Osteoporosis or Bone Metabolic Disorders: Conditions that affect bone density and quality, such as osteoporosis or Paget's disease, may compromise the stability and integration of dental implants. Bone density assessments and possibly bone grafting procedures may be necessary before implant placement.
  • History of Radiation Therapy: Patients who have undergone head or neck radiation therapy, particularly in the jaw area, may have compromised bone quality and reduced blood flow to the tissues, which can affect implant success. Prior radiation therapy requires careful evaluation and consideration of alternative treatment options.
  • Smoking and Tobacco Use: Smoking and tobacco use can impair healing, increase the risk of infection, and contribute to implant failure. Patients who smoke are encouraged to quit or reduce smoking before and after implant surgery to optimize outcomes.
  • Gum Disease (Periodontitis): Active gum disease can lead to inflammation and infection around implants, potentially causing implant failure. It's important to treat and stabilize gum disease before proceeding with implant placement.
  • Bruxism (Teeth Grinding): Chronic teeth grinding can put excessive pressure on implants and lead to mechanical complications or implant failure over time. Patients with bruxism may require a night guard or other interventions to protect their implants.
  • Medications: Certain medications, such as bisphosphonates used to treat osteoporosis, may affect bone metabolism and healing. Patients taking these medications should inform their dentist or oral surgeon to assess any potential risks or adjustments to treatment.
  • Psychological Factors: Anxiety or fear of dental procedures can impact treatment outcomes. Patients who experience significant dental anxiety may benefit from discussing sedation options or psychological support to ensure a comfortable and successful implant experience.
  • Pregnancy: While dental implants are generally safe, elective procedures are typically postponed during pregnancy due to potential risks and changes in health status. It's advisable to wait until after pregnancy and breastfeeding to undergo implant surgery.
Before proceeding with dental implants, it's crucial to disclose your complete medical history, including any medications you are taking and any underlying health conditions, to your dentist or oral surgeon. They will evaluate your suitability for implants, discuss potential risks and alternatives, and create a personalized treatment plan to achieve the best possible outcome for your dental health.
