How is flap operation performed?

Local anesthesia is typically used during gum surgery, ensuring that patients do not feel pain or discomfort throughout the procedure. During treatment, the gum tissue may be carefully lifted, cut, or reshaped depending on the needs of the patient.

In gum flap surgery, the surgeon gains access to the diseased tissues through a small incision. Once the infected areas are removed, dental tartar and plaque are thoroughly cleaned. If necessary, artificial grafts are placed to support tissue regeneration. After the treatment is completed, the incision is closed with stitches to promote proper healing.

How long does gum surgery take to heal?

The healing time for gum surgery can vary depending on the type of surgery performed and the individual patient's condition. Generally, here are some typical timeframes for healing: Initial Healing: The first phase of healing, where the gum tissue begins to reattach and close the surgical wounds, usually takes about 1-2 weeks. During this period, patients may experience some swelling, discomfort, and minor bleeding. Complete Healing: Full recovery and maturation of the gum tissue can take several weeks to a few months. The exact duration depends on the complexity of the surgery and the patient's overall health and adherence to post-operative care instructions. Factors Influencing Healing Time Type of Surgery:
  • Scaling and Root Planing: This non-surgical procedure may result in minor discomfort and generally heals within a few days to a week.
  • Flap Surgery: This common periodontal procedure usually requires about 1-2 weeks for initial healing and up to a month or more for complete healing.
  • Gum Grafting: Healing can take 2-4 weeks for initial recovery, with full healing taking up to 6-8 weeks.
  • Laser Gum Surgery: Recovery is often quicker, with initial healing in 1-2 weeks and full recovery in a few weeks.
Patient's Health: Overall health, including conditions like diabetes or immune deficiencies, can affect the healing process.
  • Oral Hygiene: Maintaining good oral hygiene as instructed by the dentist can significantly speed up healing.
  • Post-Operative Care: Following the dentist's instructions regarding diet, activity, and oral care is crucial for a smooth recovery.
Post-Surgery Care Tips
  • Oral Hygiene: Brush gently and avoid the surgical area initially; use any prescribed mouthwash or saline rinses.
  • Diet: Eat soft foods and avoid hot, spicy, or hard foods that could irritate the surgical site.
  • Medication: Take prescribed pain relievers and antibiotics as directed.
  • Activity: Avoid strenuous activities and smoking, as they can impede healing.
It's important to follow up with your dentist for post-operative appointments to ensure proper healing and address any concerns promptly. If you experience severe pain, excessive bleeding, or signs of infection, contact your dentist immediately.
