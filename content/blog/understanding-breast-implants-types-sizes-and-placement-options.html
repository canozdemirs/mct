What are breast implants?

Breast implants are prostheses, or artificial devices, that are surgically implanted into your breasts. Silicone shells that are either filled with silicone gel or saline—sterile salt water—are used to create breast implants. Physicians referred to as plastic surgeons place breast implants. When a breast is lost to cancer, you may decide to get breast implants. It's possible that you are in good health but would like to alter the size or form of your breasts. When a patient with breast cancer has breast implants, it is known as breast reconstruction. When a healthy individual gets breast implants to alter the size or form of their breasts, the procedure is known as breast augmentation. A "boob job" is another term for breast augmentation.

What is the purpose of breast implants?

A common goal of breast implant recipients is to increase their breast size. There are various reasons why you might desire to increase the size of your breasts, such as:
  • Regaining one's former breast size. Breast size can decrease with aging, weight reduction, or pregnancy.
  • Reshaping breasts that are asymmetrical in terms of size or form. Breast asymmetry is typical, but if you have tissue damage in one of your breasts before puberty, it could be more noticeable. 
  • Breast reconstruction following a mastectomy (breast removal). Breast implants give women who had their breasts removed due to cancer or other significant health conditions a sense of completion and femininity again.
  • Increasing self-esteem. Your body image and sense of self-worth can both be enhanced by breast implants.

Where does breast implants placed?

Many patients are unaware that they have a choice in where their breast implants are placed. Implant placement can occur either under or above the muscle, and various aspects are taken into account to establish the best location for each patient, including:
  • Your body type
  • Your current state of health
  • The implants' true size (in centimeters)
Board-certified and experienced plastic surgeons with a high volume of breast augmentation surgeries will be able to direct the best position to determine optimal implant placement.

The sub-glandular placement

They are also called "over-the-muscle" implantation, which involves positioning the implant between the breast tissue portions that are already there and the chest muscle. The patient will be able to breastfeed in the future thanks to the over-the-muscle location, which places the implant underneath the breast glands.
  • Advantages: Immediately after surgery, patients report decreased pain and less movement of the implants during physical activities.
  • Disadvantages: The appearance could seem more phony. Furthermore, readings during mammography could be less precise. Patients may also be more likely to develop capsular contracture, a condition in which the scar tissue or capsule that typically surrounds the implant tightens and presses on it.

The submuscular placement

The alternative is referred to as submuscular, or placing the breast implant beneath the muscle. Using this method, the implant is partly positioned beneath the pectoralis major, a chest muscle.
  • Advantages: Patients receive more accurate mammograms. Furthermore, a more natural-looking appearance and a lower incidence of capsular contracture are often produced by the submuscular placement.
  • Disadvantages: After surgery, some people report slightly increased discomfort and a longer recovery period.

What are the other important factors for breast implant placement?

When it comes to the ideal location of the implants during breast augmentation treatments, the muscles in the chest are crucial. The amount of muscle that each patient possesses varies, and this may influence the optimal placement technique—sub-glandular or submuscular. Every patient's anatomy needs to be taken into account. Women with particularly large or powerful chest muscles, for instance, are most suited for implant placement over the muscles. One explanation for this is that as the chest muscles contract and flex, larger chest muscles tend to distort the breasts. Subglandular placement is suggested in these circumstances. The quantity of tissue present in the breast region before surgery will influence the implantation site for best outcomes following surgery, in addition to muscle mass and density. The pectoralis muscle is unnecessary when there is a substantial quantity of breast tissue since there will be sufficient soft tissue coverage. You should talk about the location of the breast implants and determine which option—under or over the muscle—will be ideal for you as you choose your breast surgeon and have your consultation regarding the treatment.

What are the types of breast implants?

  • Saline breast implants: Sterile salt water is used to fill saline breast implants. A saline implant will collapse if the implant shell leaks, allowing the saline to be absorbed and naturally removed by the body. The FDA has approved saline breast implants for augmentation in women who are 18 years of age or older.
  • Structured breast implants: They offer a consistent shape, firmness, and feel. Sterilized salt water is used to fill structured implants, which also have an interior structure designed to give the implant a more natural feel. 
  • Silicone breast implants: Silicone gel is used to fill silicone breast implants. The gel has a somewhat more realistic breast tissue feel to it. The gel may seep into the breast implant pocket or stay inside the implant shell if the implant leaks. A silicone gel-filled implant that leaks won't crumble. If you decide on silicone implants, you might need to schedule routine check-ups with your plastic surgeon to ensure the implants are operating as intended. Breast implants can be screened for health using an MRI or ultrasound. For women who are 22 years of age or older, silicone breast implants are FDA-approved for augmentation.
  • Gummy bear breast implants: Because form-stable implants keep their shape even if the implant shell cracks, they are commonly referred to as "gummy bear" breast implants. Compared to conventional silicone gel implants, the implant's silicone gel has a thicker consistency. Additionally, these implants are more rigid than conventional implants. Gummy bear-shaped breast implants taper toward the top and have a greater projection at the bottom. Further surgery may be necessary to rectify an atypical appearance of the breast resulting from the rotation of a shaped implant. The skin incision needed to place gummy bear implants must be significantly longer.
  • Round breast implants: Compared to form-stable implants, round breast implants typically give the appearance of fuller breasts. Even more projection can be attained with higher-profile alternatives. Round implants are less likely to rotate out of position since they are uniformly shaped.
  • Smooth breast implants: The smoothest feeling breast implants are. They may move more naturally if they move in tandem with the breast implant pocket. Under the skin, smooth implants may have some discernible or felt rippling.
  • Textured breast implants: Texturally enhanced breast implants reduce the likelihood of movement and repositioning by forming scar tissue that adheres to the implant. There is some benefit to texturing in lowering the possibility of a taut scar capsule.

What are the most natural looking breast implants?

In 2012, the FDA in the United States authorized the use of a silicone gel breast implant that was incredibly cohesive. People often refer to these implants as "gummy bear breast implants." Like gummy bear candies, they hold their shape when cut in half. Different shapes are also available for breast implants. Some kinds of breast implants resemble rounded doughnuts without holes in them. Others resemble a teardrop or a natural breast, with a narrower top and a rounded bottom. They therefore resemble real breasts more. The outside surface of circular breast implants can be textured or smooth. But teardrop-shaped implants often just have a textured casing. Shells with texture lessen the possibility that they will rotate and appear abnormal.

What are the sizes of breast implants?

When anybody, especially doctors, discusses the size of breast implants, they often mean the implants' volume in cubic centimeters. Given that it indicates the amount of space the implant occupies within the body, this is the most significant measurement of breast implant size. The volume range of various-sized breast implants is displayed in this chart:
  • Small size: 150-200 cc
  • Medium size: 300-350 cc
  • Large size: Over 400 cc
  • Very Large size: Up to 800 cc
The diameter of breast implants is a crucial factor that affects both the end product's shape and how well it fits your body, in addition to its volume. The diameter, which is also known as the base width, ranges from 7 to 17 centimeters. Your surgeon will go over implant diameter with you and guide you in selecting the best alternative to achieve a final result that is both visually acceptable and natural-looking.
