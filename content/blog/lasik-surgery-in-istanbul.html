Laser Treatment for Eye Problems

Eye defects are among the health problems that make daily life difficult and disturb individuals. These defects, which necessitate the use of glasses or contact lenses for a healthy vision, negatively affect the comfort of the people, and the use of glasses is a visually undesirable situation for many people. LASIK stands for Laser-Assisted in Situ Keratomileusis. It's a type of refractive surgery that reshapes the cornea, the clear dome at the front of your eye. By changing the cornea's curvature, LASIK can correct vision problems like nearsightedness, farsightedness, and astigmatism. Today, with the developing medical technology, it has become possible to treat eye defects and to eliminate the necessity of wearing glasses with the help of laser applications for patients with suitable profiles. While vision disorders such as nearsightedness or astigmatism at various degrees can be treated with Excimer laser applications, some problems related to the transparent layer of the eye can be treated using the same technique. The treatment of eye defects with the help of laser technique, which is simple and does not require surgical interventions, provides a permanent improvement that continues for life and relieves patients both physiologically and psychologically.

What is LASIK?

LASIK; It is the name of an operation in which visual defects such as myopia, hyperopia, and astigmatism are corrected with a special laser called an excimer laser. With this laser, the cornea layer in the outermost part of the eye is reshaped and the light is focused on the retina. Are There Risks to Treating Eye Defects with Laser?

Many individuals who are researching laser treatment options for eye defects have questions such as whether the procedures are risky and whether there is a possibility of reoccurrence of eye defects. One of the frequently asked questions about this subject is whether there is a risk of returning the glasses number. Just as healthy eyes may deteriorate later, it is possible to develop defects later in laser-treated eyes. However, this risk is very low when applied to people over the age of 20 whose progression has stopped. Hyperopia (nearsightedness) defects treated with laser are much less likely to recur than myopic defects (farsightedness). Likewise, the biggest reason why laser eye treatment is not recommended for individuals under the age of 18 is that the probability of recurrence is much higher than in adults. There is no obstacle to performing other eye-related surgical operations in patients treated with laser treatment techniques that have been safely applied for more than 20 years around the world. Who is not suitable for laser treatment?

There are some patient groups that are not suitable for laser treatment, except for individuals under the age of 20 and those whose eye number continues to progress. These can be listed as follows:
  • Those with dry eyes,
  • Those with corneal deformities,
  • Those who have an active eye infection,
  • Those with thin corneas,
  • Pregnant women,
  • Those who have diseases that affect the immune system such as rheumatic diseases, diabetes, or that may prevent eye healing are not suitable for laser treatment.
Another issue that patients who want to have laser eye treatment are wondering is how many degrees of laser treatment can be done in the eyes. The point to be known in this regard is that the structure of the eye should be appropriate rather than the degree for laser treatment. If the thickness of the cornea and the structural condition of the eye are suitable, there is no obstacle to laser treatment in most cases. In patients with suitable eye structure, laser treatment can be applied up to 10 degrees in case of myopia, up to plus 5 degrees in hyperopia, and up to 5 degrees in astigmatism. However, it may not be possible to reach these degrees in patients with insufficient corneal thickness. In patients with suitable eye structures, a laser can be applied more than once.
