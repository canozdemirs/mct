What is Rhinoplasty Surgery?

Rhinoplasty, commonly known as a nose job, is a surgical procedure designed to reshape or refine the nose. While many people choose rhinoplasty for cosmetic reasons — such as adjusting the nose’s size, shape, or proportions — the surgery can also address medical concerns, including breathing difficulties caused by structural problems within the nasal passages.

There are two primary types of rhinoplasty. Cosmetic rhinoplasty focuses on enhancing the appearance of the nose by reshaping its structure to achieve a more balanced facial profile. Functional rhinoplasty, on the other hand, aims to correct internal issues such as a deviated septum, improving airflow and overall breathing function.

During the procedure, the surgeon may reshape bone, cartilage, or skin depending on the patient’s needs and aesthetic goals. Rhinoplasty is typically performed under general anesthesia. Recovery varies from person to person, but swelling usually decreases over several weeks, with the final results becoming more noticeable as the nose continues to settle into its new shape.

What are the Types of Rhinoplasty?

Rhinoplasty procedures can be categorized based on their purpose and approach. Here are the main types: Cosmetic Rhinoplasty Reduction Rhinoplasty: Reduces the size of the nose by reshaping the bone or cartilage. Augmentation Rhinoplasty: Enhances nasal features using cartilage or other materials to add structure or correct depressions. Ethnic Rhinoplasty: Addresses specific aesthetic goals for people from diverse ethnic backgrounds, respecting unique nasal features. Teen Rhinoplasty: Performed on teenagers to correct cosmetic or functional issues, typically after growth has stabilized (around 15-16 years for girls, 17-18 for boys). Functional Rhinoplasty Septoplasty: Corrects a deviated septum, which may obstruct airflow and cause breathing difficulties. Turbinate Reduction: Reduces enlarged turbinates (nasal structures that warm and humidify air) to improve breathing. Reconstructive Rhinoplasty Used to restore form and function after trauma, injury, or previous surgery complications. This often involves cartilage grafts from the rib, ear, or other sources to rebuild the nasal structure. Revision (Secondary) Rhinoplasty Performed when a previous rhinoplasty did not yield satisfactory results, or complications arose. It is often more complex due to scar tissue and altered anatomy. Closed vs. Open Rhinoplasty Closed Rhinoplasty: Incisions are made within the nostrils, leaving no visible scars, but it provides limited access to the nose structure. Open Rhinoplasty: Involves a small incision on the columella (the tissue between the nostrils) to lift the skin, offering better visibility and precision, particularly for complex reshaping.
