How does Finasteride function?

This medication inhibits the production of DHT, a biological hormone that stimulates the growth of the prostate. Finasteride inhibits the 5-alpha-reductase enzyme's activity. This enzyme changes testosterone into another hormone, which in turn causes men to develop enlarged prostates or experience hair loss. As a result, the prostate will lessen and the scalp's hair will increase in growth. Compared to men with a lot of hair, individuals with androgenetic alopecia (male pattern baldness) have smaller hair follicles and higher levels of DHT in their scalps. In males who are genetically susceptible, finasteride lowers DHT levels in the scalp and appears to stop a major factor in the onset of androgenetic alopecia. The group of drugs known as 5-alpha reductase inhibitors includes finasteride. Therefore, Finasteride acts by inhibiting Type II 5'-reductase, an enzyme that transforms the hormone testosterone into dihydrotestosterone (DHT). As a result, levels of testosterone rise while those of dihydrotestosterone fall. Dihydrotestosterone excess has been linked to prostate cancer and benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH). Remember that the prostate and scalp effects of finasteride last just as long as the medicine is used. If it is stopped, the prostate will begin to expand, and hair loss will follow.
