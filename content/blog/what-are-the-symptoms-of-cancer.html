What Causes Cancer?

Cancer is caused by mutations to the DNA. These genetic mutations can lead to uncontrolled cell growth and the formation of tumors. The causes of these mutations can be varied and often involve a combination of genetic, environmental, and lifestyle factors. Here are the main causes of cancer: Genetic Factors
  • Inherited Mutations:
    • Some people inherit genetic mutations from their parents that increase their risk of certain types of cancer. Examples include mutations in the BRCA1 and BRCA2 genes, which are associated with a higher risk of breast and ovarian cancers.
  • Family History:
    • A family history of cancer can indicate a genetic predisposition to cancer. However, not all cancers that run in families are caused by inherited mutations; they can also result from shared environmental or lifestyle factors.
Environmental and Occupational Factors
  • Radiation Exposure:
    • Exposure to ionizing radiation, such as from X-rays, radon gas, and radioactive substances, can damage DNA and increase the risk of cancer.
  • Chemical Carcinogens:
    • Certain chemicals can cause cancer. Examples include asbestos, benzene, formaldehyde, and tobacco smoke. Occupational exposure to these chemicals can significantly increase cancer risk.
  • Pollution:
    • Air and water pollution, including exposure to harmful substances like arsenic, can contribute to cancer development.
Lifestyle Factors
  • Tobacco Use:
    • Smoking and the use of tobacco products are the leading causes of lung cancer and are linked to many other cancers, including mouth, throat, pancreas, bladder, and kidney cancers.
  • Diet and Physical Activity:
    • Poor diet, lack of physical activity, and obesity are linked to an increased risk of several types of cancer, including colon, breast, endometrial, and kidney cancers.
  • Alcohol Consumption:
    • Heavy alcohol consumption is associated with an increased risk of cancers of the mouth, throat, esophagus, liver, colon, and breast.
Aging
  • Age:
    • The risk of cancer increases with age. This is partly because the body's ability to repair DNA damage diminishes over time, and the cumulative exposure to carcinogens grows with age.
Understanding the various causes of cancer helps in adopting preventive measures and early detection strategies to reduce the risk of developing cancer. What are the Symptoms of Cancer?

Cancer symptoms can vary widely depending on the type of cancer, its location, and its stage. However, some common signs and symptoms may include:
  • General Symptoms
  • Specific Symptoms Based on Cancer Type
  • Other Symptoms

General Symptoms

Unexplained Weight Loss:
    • Losing weight without trying can be a sign of cancer. It is often seen in cancers of the stomach, pancreas, esophagus, and lung.
Fever:
    • Persistent or recurrent fever can be an early sign of cancer, especially blood-related cancers such as leukemia or lymphoma.
Fatigue:
    • Extreme tiredness that doesn’t improve with rest can be an early symptom of some cancers like leukemia or colon cancer.
Pain:
    • Persistent pain in certain areas can be a symptom of cancer, such as headaches in brain cancer or back pain in ovarian cancer.

Specific Symptoms Based on Cancer Type

Skin Changes:
    • Changes in the skin, including new moles, sores that don’t heal, or jaundice, can indicate skin cancer or cancers that have spread to the skin.
Changes in Bowel or Bladder Habits:
    • Long-term constipation, diarrhea, changes in stool size, or urinary problems can be symptoms of colon, bladder, or prostate cancer.
Persistent Cough or Hoarseness:
    • A persistent cough, chest pain, hoarseness, or trouble breathing can be signs of lung or throat cancer.
Unusual Bleeding or Discharge:
    • Unusual bleeding, such as blood in the stool, urine, or from the vagina, can indicate cancers of the colon, bladder, or cervix.
Lumps or Thickening:
    • Lumps or thickening in the breast, testicles, lymph nodes, or other body parts can be early signs of cancer.
Difficulty Swallowing or Indigestion:
    • Persistent indigestion or difficulty swallowing can be symptoms of esophageal, stomach, or throat cancer.
 

Other Symptoms

Skin Changes:
    • Any changes in the skin, such as darkening, yellowing, or redness, sores that don't heal, or changes to existing moles or warts, can be indicators of skin cancer or internal cancers that affect skin appearance.
Night Sweats:
    • Severe night sweats can sometimes be a symptom of certain types of cancers, particularly lymphomas.
Swelling or Persistent Bloating:
    • Abdominal bloating, especially if persistent and unexplained, can be a sign of ovarian cancer or other gastrointestinal cancers.
Neurological Symptoms:
    • Headaches, seizures, vision or hearing changes, or other neurological symptoms can be signs of brain cancer or cancers that have metastasized to the brain.
Persistent Sore Throat:
    • A sore throat that doesn’t go away, difficulty swallowing, or a lump in the throat could be signs of throat or esophageal cancer.
   

What Should We Do If We Have Cancer Symptoms?

If you experience symptoms that you suspect might be related to cancer, it is important to take prompt and appropriate action. Here are the steps you should follow:
  • Monitor Your Symptoms
  • Schedule a Doctor's Appointment
  • Prepare for the Appointment
  • Diagnostic Tests
  • Follow Up on Test Results
  • Treatment Planning
  • Lifestyle and Support
  • Regular Monitoring
  • Stay Informed
If you have symptoms that could be related to cancer, it’s essential to act promptly by consulting with a healthcare professional. Early diagnosis and treatment can significantly improve outcomes.
