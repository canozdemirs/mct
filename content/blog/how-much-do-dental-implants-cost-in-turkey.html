Dental Implant Risks

Like any surgery, dental implant placement presents some health risks. Problems are rare, and when they do occur, are usually minor and easily treatable. Dental implant complications may include:
  • Infection at the implant site
  • Injury or damage to the surrounding teeth, blood vessels, or jaw
  • Implant rejection
  • Sinus problems when improperly placed in the upper jaw
These risks can be reduced when the procedure is performed by an experienced surgeon who properly prepares the treatment site and places the implants in the proper location. For example, bone grafts can increase the implant's likelihood of success, as well as specialized dental implant systems.

Eleven Benefits of Dental Implants

  1. Dental Implants Look Like Real Teeth

Once the dental implant is fitted with a prosthesis, the results look very natural. The prosthesis lies flush with the gums so no one can see which is your natural tooth and which is being restored.

  1. Dental Implants Behave Like Real Teeth

One of the greatest advantages of dental implants is that they behave like natural teeth and provide strength and function unmatched by other restorations. Since the dental implant itself is firmly embedded in your jawbone like a natural root, it provides stability to the denture.

  1. Dental Implants can Prevent Bone Loss

One of the side effects of tooth loss is that you start to lose bone mass around the site of the missing tooth. This is because there are no roots left there to promote bone ossification/strengthening. Placing a dental implant in your jawbone stimulates new bone growth through a process called osseointegration. This can help prevent future bone loss.

  1. Dental Implants Support Your Facial Structure

Bone loss can make people who have lost teeth look sunken and aged around the mouth, which in turn affects the structure of the entire face. By helping maintain a healthy jawbone, dental implants can help maintain the structure of your face.

  1. Long-Lasting Dental Implants

Like your natural teeth, dental implants have the potential to last a lifetime with proper care.
