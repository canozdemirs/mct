What is a boob job?

A boob job is a cosmetic surgical procedure known as breast augmentation or breast enlargement. In this procedure, a surgeon enhances the size and shape of a person's breasts by inserting implants under the breast tissue or chest muscles. These implants are typically filled with saline solution or silicone gel. People may choose to undergo a boob job for various reasons, including to enhance their appearance, improve their self-confidence, or restore breast volume lost due to factors such as pregnancy, weight loss, or aging.

What are the risks of a boob job?

Risks associated with breast augmentation include:
  • Scar tissue (capsular contracture) that warps the form of the breast implant
  • Breast discomfort
  • Infection
  • Modifications in breast and nipple feeling
  • Position adjustments of implants
  • Rupture or leaking of an implant
To address these issues, more surgery may be necessary to replace or remove the implants.

What is the procedure for boob job surgery?

Your plastic surgeon will create a single incision in one of three locations to install the breast implant:
  • The inframammary crease, located beneath your breasts
  • Under your arm (axillary)
  • The area periareolar to your nipple
The surgeon will separate your breast tissue from the muscles and connective tissue of your chest after making an incision. As a result, a pocket is formed either in front of or behind the pectoral muscle, which is the largest muscle in the chest wall. The implant will be centered behind your nipple by the surgeon after being inserted into this space. After being placed, sterile salt water is poured into saline implants, which are first placed empty. Silicone gel comes pre-filled in silicone implants. After placing the implant, the surgeon will bind the wound using surgical tape and skin glue and close it, usually with stitches.
