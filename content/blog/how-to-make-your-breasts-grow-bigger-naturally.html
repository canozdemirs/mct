What factors determine your breast size?

Genetics plays a major role in determining breast size. Women often consider breast volume or fullness while considering breast size. Your breast size appears to be influenced by several other factors, including your chest circumference. Bone structure and breast volume are physical traits that can run in families. Several variables other than inheritance determine breast size. Among them are,

Age and weight

Your breasts will look different in your teen years than they would in your later years. They might get bigger or smaller, and there might be some drooping. Gaining or losing weight can cause major changes in cup size and torso circumference. Your bra size will likely increase or decrease if you lose or gain over twenty pounds.  

The reproductive cycle and hormones

When you reach puberty and your ovaries begin to produce estrogen, your breast tissue begins to swell up with fat. Right before your menstruation, estrogen and progesterone cause the breast ducts and milk glands to briefly expand. One of the earliest indications of pregnancy is enlarged breasts. Prolactin, progesterone, and estrogens promote breast growth and milk production. Throughout your pregnancy, your breasts will continue to enlarge as they get ready to give birth. A decrease in estrogen throughout menopause may result in your breasts becoming less elastic and smaller.

What are the surgical options to grow breasts?

A common surgical operation that enhances the size and form of your breasts is called breast augmentation. You can use fat transfer or breast implants to increase the size of your breasts. The two primary forms of breast augmentation are breast implants and fat transfer augmentation. There are several possibilities depending on how you want your breasts to feel and look within those two categories.  

Breast Implants

Only a specific age range is permitted by the FDA for some breast implants. The most popular kind of breast augmentation is breast implants. Options for breast implants include:
  • Saline breast implants: Sterile saline, or salt water, is used to fill these implants. In the unlikely event that the implant breaks inside your breast, the saline will be absorbed by your body and eliminated normally. Saline breast implants having an internal structure that makes them feel more natural are known as structured saline implants. They are filled with sterile saline or salt water.
  • Silicone breast implants: Silicone gel is used to make silicone gel breast implants. The gel might seep into your breast or remain inside the implant's shell if it broke. If you receive silicone implants, you might need to schedule routine check-ups with your plastic surgeon to ensure the implants are functioning well.
 

Fat Transfer

Fat transfer breast augmentation: Your surgeon will utilize liposuction to remove fat from one part of your body, which will then be injected into your breasts. Those who desire a somewhat modest increase in their breast size typically choose for this kind of augmentation. Your surgeon will often remove fat tissue from one of the following locations:
  • Your belly
  • Your flanks (the sides and lower back of your abdomen).
  • Your back
  • Your thighs

What is the procedure?

The procedure for breast augmentation involves numerous steps. Here is a breakdown of the procedures.  

Anesthesia

During the procedure, your surgeon will either use IV sedation or general anesthetic, which will put you to sleep. This is something you and your surgeon will decide together.  

The incision

There are various methods for performing breast augmentation. The surgery can be carried out by your surgeon:
  • Via the inframammary fold, the crease located beneath your breast
  • Along the periareolar incision, which is the border of your areola
  • Through the area under your armpit (transaxillary technique)
Before your procedure, your surgeon will go over these potential approaches with you. Together, you will decide which one best meets your needs.  

Implant insertion

Your surgeon will implant the device in either the pectoral muscle, which is located behind your breast muscle, or under your breast tissue. The type of implant you select and the extent to which you are enlarging your breasts are two of the variables that determine where the implants will be placed. Together with your surgeon, you can weigh the advantages of each approach and decide which one is best for you.  

Closing the incision

Your surgeon will close the incision sites by sewing them together after placing your implants. Drainage tubes may also be used by your surgeon. Observe the incision site follow-up instructions provided by your surgeon. You can be sent home with a surgical bra on and your breasts wrapped in a gauze bandage.
