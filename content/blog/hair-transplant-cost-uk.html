How do hair transplants work?

The concept behind hair transplantation is quite simple. Small healthy hairs are removed from parts of the male body that are genetically resistant to baldness (often from the back of the head). These hairs are then transplanted or "grafted" into the bald area under local anaesthesia. Once implanted, they are ready to grow back to replace lost hair and will theoretically continue to be resistant to baldness. Usually, 500-3000 hair follicles are transplanted in one surgical session. Each of these hairs needs to be transplanted separately to get a natural hairline. This means that each session can last up to eight hours and can be very painful even under sedation. Depending on the technique used, scarring may occur in the donor area of ​​the scalp. What are the different types of transplants?

There are two types of hair transplants – FUT and FUE. Both FUE and FUT are procedures performed by highly specialized surgeons and although the basic process is very similar for the two, there are some key differences. FUE means follicular unit extraction and is the most secretive method of hair transplantation as you only need to shave the back and sides of the head to prepare for the procedure. In addition, this method is usually preferred because it is minimally invasive, and the scars may be less visible than other methods. FUT, which means a follicular unit transfer, involves the surgeon isolating a small piece of follicle-rich scalp tissue, removing the tape, and transferring it to the bald or thinning area. This area is then immediately closed with special sutures in a procedure known as trichophyte closure. The FUT procedure often requires you to shave more hair than the FUE procedure, and the scar is linear, making it more visible. However, it is still a very popular choice.
