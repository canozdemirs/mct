What is Dental Crowns?

Dental crowns, also known as caps, are dental prosthetic devices that are placed over damaged or decayed teeth.
  • Purpose: Crowns are used to cover a tooth that is weakened by decay, cracks, or large fillings. They protect the tooth from further damage and restore its function.
  • Materials: Crowns can be made from various materials including porcelain, ceramic, metal alloys, or a combination of these materials. The choice of material depends on factors such as the location of the tooth, the function of the tooth, and aesthetic considerations.
  • Procedure: Getting a dental crown usually requires two visits to the dentist. During the first visit, the dentist prepares the tooth by removing a layer of its outer surface to make room for the crown. An impression of the tooth is then taken and sent to a dental laboratory where the crown is custom-made. A temporary crown is placed on the tooth to protect it while the permanent crown is being fabricated. During the second visit, the permanent crown is cemented onto the prepared tooth.
  • Types: There are different types of crowns available, such as all-ceramic (porcelain-based), porcelain-fused-to-metal (PFM), all-metal (gold or other metal alloys), and zirconia crowns. Each type has its own advantages and considerations.
  • Benefits: Crowns not only strengthen and protect damaged teeth but also improve their appearance, shape, and alignment. They can restore proper biting and chewing function and enhance overall oral health.
  • Care: Once in place, crowns require regular brushing, flossing, and dental check-ups, just like natural teeth, to ensure longevity and maintain oral hygiene.

How Dental Crowns Work in Turkey?

Dental crowns work by covering or "capping" a damaged or decayed tooth to restore its shape, size, strength, and function.
  • Assessment and Preparation:
    • Your dentist will first assess the condition of your tooth to determine if a crown is necessary.
    • If it's decided that a crown is needed, the dentist will prepare the tooth by removing a layer of its outer surface. This reshaping provides enough space for the crown to fit securely over the tooth.
  • Impression:
    • After the tooth is prepared, your dentist will take an impression (mold) of the tooth. This impression is used as a model for creating the custom-made crown. It ensures that the crown will fit precisely over your tooth and align properly with your bite.
  • Temporary Crown:
    • While the permanent crown is being fabricated (which can take a few weeks), your dentist may place a temporary crown over the prepared tooth. This temporary crown protects the tooth and prevents sensitivity.
  • Permanent Crown Placement:
    • The dentist will remove the temporary crown and check the fit and color of the permanent crown.
    • If everything is satisfactory, the permanent crown is cemented into place over your prepared tooth.
  • Adjustments:
    • The dentist may make minor adjustments to ensure that the crown fits comfortably and aligns properly with your other teeth.
    • They will also check your bite to ensure that it is natural and comfortable.
  • Final Polishing:
    • Once the crown is securely in place and any adjustments are made, the dentist will polish the crown to give it a smooth and natural appearance.
After the crown is placed, it functions like a natural tooth, restoring the tooth's strength and appearance. It allows you to chew and bite normally, protecting the underlying tooth from further damage or decay. Proper oral hygiene, including regular brushing, flossing, and dental check-ups, will help maintain the crown and the health of the surrounding teeth and gums.

What are the Types of Dental Crowns?

The choice of crown depends on factors such as the location of the tooth, the amount of natural tooth remaining, functional requirements, aesthetic preferences, and budget. Here are the main types of dental crowns:
  • Metal Crowns:
    • Gold Alloy Crowns: These crowns are made of a gold alloy, which is a mix of gold, copper, and other metals. Gold crowns are highly durable, gentle on opposing teeth, and require minimal tooth structure removal. They are often used for molars and teeth in the back of the mouth.
    • Other Metal Alloy Crowns: These include crowns made of base metal alloys (non-precious metals), which are also strong and resistant to wear. They are usually silver or gray in color.
  • Porcelain-Fused-to-Metal (PFM) Crowns:
    • These crowns have a metal base (substructure) covered with a layer of tooth-colored porcelain. They provide a natural appearance due to the porcelain layer, which can be color-matched to adjacent teeth. However, the metal substructure may show as a dark line near the gumline in some cases.
  • All-Ceramic or All-Porcelain Crowns:
    • These crowns are made entirely of ceramic materials such as porcelain. They are highly aesthetic because they can be color-matched to blend seamlessly with natural teeth. All-ceramic crowns are a popular choice for front teeth where appearance is a primary concern. They are also a good option for patients with metal allergies.
  • Zirconia Crowns:
    • Zirconia crowns are a type of ceramic crown known for their strength and durability. They are made from zirconium oxide, a very strong material that can withstand biting and chewing forces well. Zirconia crowns are also highly aesthetic and can be color-matched to natural teeth.
  • Composite Resin Crowns:
    • These crowns are made from a tooth-colored composite resin material. They are less commonly used compared to other types of crowns due to their inferior strength and durability. They may be a temporary solution or used in certain specific situations.

Which Dental Crown Metarial is Better?

Choosing the best dental crown material depends on several factors including the location of the tooth, your oral health, budget, and aesthetic preferences. Here are some common materials used for dental crowns and their characteristics:
  • Porcelain-fused-to-metal (PFM) crowns:
    • Strength: Moderate to strong, suitable for both front and back teeth.
    • Aesthetics: Tooth-colored porcelain can be matched to adjacent teeth.
    • Durability: Can be prone to chipping over time.
    • Cost: Moderate.
  • All-ceramic or all-porcelain crowns:
    • Strength: Strong and durable, especially newer materials like zirconia.
    • Aesthetics: Excellent, very natural-looking, can be matched to surrounding teeth.
    • Durability: Very good, resistant to wear.
    • Cost: Higher than PFM crowns, but varies depending on the specific material.
  • Gold alloy crowns:
    • Strength: Extremely strong and durable.
    • Aesthetics: Gold color may not match natural teeth, primarily used for molars where appearance is less important.
    • Durability: Excellent, least likely to cause wear on opposing teeth.
    • Cost: Moderate to high.
  • Base metal alloy crowns:
    • Strength: Very strong and durable.
    • Aesthetics: Tooth-colored porcelain can be added, but not as aesthetic as all-ceramic crowns.
    • Durability: Excellent, biocompatible.
    • Cost: Moderate to high.
Factors to Consider:
  • Location of the tooth: Front or back teeth.
  • Aesthetic requirements: Matching adjacent teeth.
  • Budget: Different materials have varying costs.
  • Longevity: Some materials may last longer than others.
  • Allergies or sensitivities: Some patients may have allergies to certain metals.

For front teeth, where aesthetics are a priority, all ceramic or all porcelain crowns are often recommended for their natural and lifelike appearance. For molars or areas that require maximum strength and durability, gold alloy or base metal alloy crowns are usually preferred. Porcelain fused to-metal crowns offer a perfect balance between strength and aesthetics, making them suitable for both front and back teeth.

How Long do you need to Stay in Turkey for Dental Crowns?

The duration of your stay in Turkey for dental crowns can vary depending on several factors, including the specific treatment plan, the type of crowns you choose, and the complexity of your dental condition. Here are some general guidelines:
  • Initial Consultation: Typically, your dental treatment will start with an initial consultation where your dentist will assess your oral health, discuss treatment options, and plan the procedure. This consultation may require a day or two depending on scheduling and diagnostic needs.
  • Preparation and Impressions: After the initial consultation, if dental crowns are recommended, the dentist will prepare your teeth (if necessary), take impressions, and possibly fit temporary crowns. This step usually requires at least one visit.
  • Fabrication of Crowns: The actual fabrication of the permanent crowns can take some time. In many cases, dental laboratories require around 1-2 weeks to create custom crowns, especially for materials like ceramic or porcelain.
  • Placement of Permanent Crowns: Once the permanent crowns are ready, you will need to return to the dentist for their placement. This appointment typically involves removing temporary crowns (if placed), fitting and adjusting the permanent crowns, and cementing them in place. This appointment can usually be completed in one visit, but it depends on the number of crowns and the complexity of the fitting.
  • Follow-up Appointments: After the crowns are placed, your dentist may schedule a follow-up appointment to ensure the crowns are functioning well and your bite is comfortable. This appointment is usually brief and may not require an extended stay.
Considering these steps, most patients traveling to Turkey specifically for dental crowns may plan for a stay of about 1-2 weeks. This allows sufficient time for consultations, preparations, crown fabrication, and placement. It's important to discuss the timeline with your dental provider in Turkey to get a more accurate estimate based on your specific treatment plan and needs.

How long do Dental Crowns Last?

The lifespan of dental crowns can vary depending on several factors, including the type of crown material used, oral hygiene practices, biting forces, and habits like teeth grinding (bruxism). Here’s a general overview of how long different types of dental crowns typically last:
  • Metal Crowns: Gold alloy crowns and other metal alloy crowns are known for their durability and longevity. They can often last the longest among crown types, typically 15 years to 30 years or more with proper care.
  • Porcelain-Fused-to-Metal (PFM) Crowns: These crowns combine a metal substructure with a porcelain outer layer for aesthetics. The lifespan of PFM crowns is generally around 10 to 15 years. However, the porcelain layer may wear down over time, and there is a slight risk of the metal substructure becoming visible near the gumline.
  • All-Ceramic or All-Porcelain Crowns: These crowns are highly aesthetic and can closely mimic natural teeth in appearance. With advancements in ceramic materials, their durability has improved significantly. All-ceramic crowns can last about 10 to 15 years or more, depending on the specific material used and how well they are cared for.
  • Zirconia Crowns: Zirconia crowns are known for their strength and durability. They can withstand biting and chewing forces well and typically last 10 to 15 years or more, similar to all-ceramic crowns.
  • Composite Resin Crowns: Composite resin crowns are generally less durable compared to other types of crowns and may need replacement more frequently. They are often used as temporary crowns or for specific cases where aesthetics are not a primary concern.
Factors Influencing Longevity:
  • Oral Hygiene: Good oral hygiene practices, including regular brushing, flossing, and dental check-ups, can help extend the lifespan of dental crowns.
  • Biting Forces: Avoiding habits like teeth grinding (bruxism) or using teeth to open packages can prevent premature wear or damage to crowns.
  • Diet: Limiting consumption of hard foods or items that could potentially damage crowns (like ice or hard candies) can also contribute to their longevity.
  • Dental Care: Regular visits to the dentist for professional cleanings and check-ups can help detect any issues with crowns early on and ensure they are properly maintained.
It's important to note that individual experiences with dental crowns may vary. Your dentist will provide specific care instructions based on the type of crown you have and monitor their condition during regular dental visits. If a crown becomes loose, damaged, or shows signs of wear, it may need to be replaced earlier than expected to maintain oral health and function.

What are the Benefits of Dental Crowns?

Dental crowns offer several benefits, making them a versatile and valuable treatment option in dentistry. Here are the key benefits of dental crowns:
  • Restoration of Tooth Structure: Crowns are used to restore teeth that have been significantly damaged by decay, fractures, or large fillings. They cover the entire visible portion of the tooth above the gum line, restoring its shape, size, and strength.
  • Protection: Crowns provide a protective cover for weak or damaged teeth, preventing further deterioration and potential fractures. This is especially important for teeth that have undergone root canal treatment or have large fillings.
  • Improved Function: Crowns restore the function of the tooth, allowing you to chew and bite properly. They distribute the forces of chewing evenly over the restored tooth, which helps prevent undue stress on other teeth.
  • Aesthetic Enhancement: Dental crowns can improve the appearance of teeth significantly. They are crafted to match the color, shape, and size of your natural teeth, ensuring a natural-looking smile. This makes them an excellent choice for visible front teeth.
  • Longevity: With proper care and oral hygiene, dental crowns can last many years. The lifespan of a crown depends on the material used and how well you maintain your oral health.
  • Versatility: Crowns can be used for various dental purposes, including restoring severely decayed teeth, covering discolored or misshapen teeth, supporting dental bridges, and covering dental implants.
  • Comfort: Once properly fitted, crowns feel comfortable in your mouth and function like natural teeth. They are custom-made to ensure a precise fit and bite alignment.
  • Prevention of Tooth Sensitivity: Crowns can help protect exposed tooth surfaces, reducing sensitivity to hot or cold foods and beverages.
  • Support for Dental Bridges: Crowns serve as abutments (anchors) for dental bridges, helping to bridge gaps left by missing teeth and restore your smile's appearance and function.
  • Durable Solution: Depending on the material chosen (such as porcelain, metal, or a combination), dental crowns provide a durable solution that withstands normal biting and chewing forces.
