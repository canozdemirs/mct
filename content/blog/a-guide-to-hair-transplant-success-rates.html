What is a hair transplant?

A hair transplant is a technique that fills in bald or thinning areas of the scalp with hair. It is also known as hair restoration or hair replacement, and it is usually utilized for people who have already tried other hair loss treatments. Dermatologists, who specialize in treating skin conditions, or plastic surgeons, who specialize in performing reconstructive surgery, perform hair transplants.

Which conditions can hair transplant treat?

There are numerous causes for hair loss. Hair loss can be permanent but it can also be temporary. With regards to hair loss that is permanent and brought on by:

What is FUE?

A follicular unit extraction (FUE) hair transplant involves removing individual hair follicles from your epidermis and implanting them in various parts of your body. As a result, the hair in the new region will appear thicker. FUE was designed to take the role of the "classic" follicular unit transplantation (FUT) technique. An entire piece of skin or scalp—complete with follicles—was transplanted during this procedure to the appropriate place. FUE has become more popular than FUT because the technique is less likely to produce a "hair plug" appearance when patches of skin or hair don't match the surrounding areas. It won't also create a recognizable scar, like FUT. Read more to know about A Guide to Hair Transplant Success Rates

Procedure

The FUE process operates as follows:
  • The hair around the transplant location and in the region where follicles will be extracted will be shaved by your surgeon.
  • They will eliminate the hair follicles from your skin using a micropunch tool.
  • The harvested follicles will then be placed through a series of tiny incisions made by your surgeon with a needle or other small, sharp instrument.
  • The follicles will be inserted into the incisions.
  • Your surgeon will clean the area and bandage it for healing.

What is DHI?

Similar steps are used in DHI, a modified form of FUE hair implantation. A section of your scalp, typically the back of your head, will have hair follicles removed, which the surgeon will then transplant into balding areas. Eventually, new hairs will sprout from these hair follicles.

Procedure

What to anticipate throughout the procedure in general is as follows:
  • Your surgeon will shave your head and numb it with a local anesthetic.
  • An instrument with a fine point will be used by the surgeon to remove hair follicles from the back of your head.
  • The hair follicles will be placed in a pen-shaped gadget and inserted into the area of your scalp that is balding.
  • All locations will be covered with a bandage and an antibiotic cream by the surgeon.

What are different techniques for hair transplant?

Hair transplant surgery can be done in a variety of methods, including:
  • Grafting
  • Flap surgery
  • Scalp reduction
  • Tissue expansion

The most popular method of hair transplant surgery is hair grafting. Flap Surgery

The scalp has been effectively treated using flap surgery for more than 20 years. Large patches of baldness can be swiftly covered by this treatment, which is personalized for each patient.  One flap can replace up to 350 punch grafts. A flap of skin with hair on it is peeled off the surface while still attached at one end, and a portion of the bald scalp is removed. While still "tethered" to its original blood supply, the hair-bearing flap is moved into its new location and sewed into place. The use of flap surgery in combination with scalp reduction for greater crown coverage or tissue expansion for better frontal coverage and a more realistic hairline has seen major advancements in flap procedures in recent years.

Scalp Reduction

Sections of the hair-bearing scalp are moved forward or "advanced" in order to fill in a bald crown, which is why this procedure is occasionally referred to as advancement flap surgery. The purpose of scalp reduction is to conceal bald spots on the top and rear of the head. For frontal hairline covering, it is not advantageous. A section of bald scalp is removed after a local anesthetic is injected into the scalp.  Doctors frequently remove a section of the scalp in the shape of an inverted Y when a significant amount of coverage is required. Excisions can also take the form of an oval, a U, or another shape. To bring the portions of the scalp with hair together and sew them shut, the skin around the cut-out area is loosened and tugged. Tissue Reduction

This technique is frequently employed in reconstructive surgery to treat burn wounds and wounds with a large amount of skin loss by plastic surgeons. Its use in hair transplant surgery has produced impressive results, providing considerable coverage in a short period of time. In this procedure, a tissue expander, which resembles a balloon, is implanted beneath the scalp near the bald spot. Over the course of a few weeks, salt water is progressively inflated into the device, causing the skin to expand and produce new skin cells. Particularly after a few weeks, this results in a protrusion beneath the scalp that bears hair. A second surgery is carried out to pull the stretched skin over to cover the nearby bald area once the skin beneath the hair has sufficiently stretched, which typically happens two months following the first operation.

What are the side effects and risks of hair transplant?

Most hair transplant surgery side effects are temporary. On your scalp, at the donor site, or where the new hair is, you can encounter the following problems: 
  • Scabs or crust
  • Itching
  • Swelling
  • Tightness
The risks of a hair transplant are the same as those of any surgery. These include:
  • Blood loss
  • Sensitivity to anesthesia
  • Failed grafts or flaps
  • Infection
  • Scarring

How recovery proceeds?

Even though a hair transplant is relatively simple and typically takes place in a single day, the aftercare and recovery period are essential components of the treatment and must be treated as such. The surgical technique used totally determines how long the recuperation will take. In contrast to other hair transplant procedures, the FUE hair transplant's initial recovery period is brief (10–14 days) and relatively painless. This is due to the fact that the procedure itself is less invasive than FUT surgery, which removes individual hair follicles instead of a strip of skin from the donor area.
