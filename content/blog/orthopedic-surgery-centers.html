What is orthopedics?

Orthopedics is an essential branch of medicine that focuses on diagnosing, treating, and preventing conditions related to the skeletal system and interconnecting parts, which are composed of bones, muscles, joints, tendons, ligaments, and nerves. Doctors who are professionals in orthopedics are called orthopedists. Orthopedist surgeons are trained in both surgical and non-surgical techniques. Orthopedists can treat the following things:
  • Joint pains caused by arthritis
  • Neck pain
  • Bone cancer
  • Scoliosis
  • Soft tissue injuries affecting muscles, tendons, and ligaments
  • Bone fractures
Also, in addition to surgical interventions, orthopedic specialists often collaborate with other healthcare professionals, such as physical therapists, occupational therapists, and pain management specialists, to provide comprehensive care to patients. Orthopedics aims to improve patients' quality of life by restoring function, relieving pain, and enhancing mobility.

How is an orthopedic surgery conducted?

Several planned steps are involved in orthopedic surgery. Depending on the treated condition, procedure and approach can vary. However, below you can find the typical steps conducted in an orthopedic surgery:

Patient evaluation

The doctor will do a comprehensive evaluation before the surgery. The patient will tell their medical history. A physical examination will be done, and some imaging tests like X-rays, MRI scans, or CT scans may be required. In addition, a blood test may be done to see patients' overall health.

Anesthesia

Anesthesia will be administered to the patient on the day of the surgery so that they do not feel any pain. Both general anesthesia, in which the patient is unconscious the whole process, and local anesthesia, in which only a specific area is numbed, can be used depending on the condition.

Incision

Exposure of the treatment-required area will be done via incisions. Incision size and location will be determined according to the procedure and conditions.

Surgical technique

The surgeon will choose an appropriate surgical technique based on the patient's needs. For example, metal plates, screws, or rods can be used in fracture repair, prosthetic implants can be used to replace damaged joints, damaged tissue or tumors can be removed, joints can be fused together, or ligaments and tendons can be repaired.

Closing the incision

After the needed repairments and modifications have occurred, the surgeon will close the incision using sutures, staples,or surgical glue.

Dressing and bandaging

The closed incision will be covered with a sterile dressing so that it will not get any infection.

Recovery and rehabilitation

The surgical team will monitor the patient after the surgery as they wake up from anesthesia. Depending on the importance of the surgery, the patient can go home the same day or needs to stay in the hospital for a period of time. Some physical therapy or rehabilitation may be required to strengthen the treated area and to help that area regain mobility and function.

Follow-up care

To monitor the healing process, remove stitches, and make necessary adjustments to the treatment plan, follow-up appointments with the doctor are essential.
