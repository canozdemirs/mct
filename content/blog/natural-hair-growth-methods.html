What are the causes of hair loss?

The average person loses 50–100 hairs per day. The fact that new hair is growing in at the same time usually makes this invisible. When newly grown hair fails to replenish the lost hair, hair loss results. Usually, one or more of the following factors contribute to hair loss: 
  • Heredity. The most frequent cause of hair loss is an aging-related genetic disorder. Male-pattern baldness, female-pattern baldness, and androgenic alopecia are the names given to this disorder. In men, it typically manifests as receding hairlines and bald spots, whereas in women, it thins out the hair around the crown of the scalp. These patterns are usually gradual.
  • Medical disorders and hormonal fluctuations. Permanent or temporary hair loss can result from several circumstances, including hormonal changes brought on by pregnancy, childbirth, menopause, and thyroid issues. Medical disorders include ringworm infections on the scalp, trichotillomania, and alopecia areata an immune system-related ailment that causes patchy hair loss.
  • Pharmaceuticals and dietary supplements. Drugs used to treat cancer, arthritis, depression, heart issues, gout, and high blood pressure can have adverse effects including hair loss.
  • Extremely tense situation. Many people notice a generalized thinning of hair a few months following an emotional or physical trauma. This kind of hair loss happens momentarily.
  • Hairstyles and treatments. Tight hairstyles like cornrows or pigtails, as well as excessive hairstyling, can lead to traction alopecia, a kind of hair loss. Hair loss can also be brought on by permanent and hot-oil hair treatments. Permanent hair loss may result from scarring.

What are the signs for hair loss?

The appearance of hair loss might vary greatly depending on the underlying cause. It might affect your entire body or just your scalp, and it can start slowly or rapidly. Hair loss symptoms and indicators could include:
  • Progressive thinning of the crown. As people age, this is the most prevalent type of hair loss. Men's hair frequently starts to recede at their forehead hairline. In women's hair, the part usually gets wider. A receding hairline (facial fibrosing alopecia) is a trend of hair loss that is becoming more and more common in elderly women.
  • Spotty or circular bald areas. Some people experience spotty or circular bald patches on their beard, eyebrows, or scalp. Before the hair falls out, your skin can start to hurt or become irritating.
  • Abrupt shedding of hair. A psychological or physical upset may cause hair to come out. When you brush or wash your hair, or even just after giving it a little tug, handfuls of hair may fall out. Although it is transient, this kind of hair loss typically results in general hair thinning.
  • Complete baldness on the body. Hair loss can be a side effect of many medical diseases and treatments, like chemotherapy for cancer. Usually, the hair grows back.
  • Scale patches that appear all over the scalp. This is a ringworm symptom. Broken hair, redness, swelling, and occasionally seeping may accompany it.

How hair transplantation works and cost?

Hair transplantation is a medical procedure in which a professional removes grafts tiny skin fragments containing healthy hair from a donor site, usually the back of the scalp where hair is thickest. These grafts are then carefully transplanted to areas of the scalp experiencing hair loss. Once healed, the transplanted skin continues to grow hair naturally.

There are four main methods of hair transplantation:

Grafting: Individual hair follicles are extracted and transplanted to bald areas.

Scalp Reduction: Sections of bald scalp are surgically removed, and the hair-bearing scalp is stretched to cover the area.

Flap Surgery: A section of scalp with healthy hair is moved to bald areas while keeping its blood supply intact.

Tissue Expansion: A balloon-like device is inserted under the scalp to stretch hair-bearing skin, which is then used to cover bald regions.

Cost Considerations:
The cost of hair transplantation depends on factors such as the chosen method, the extent of hair loss, and the clinic’s experience. It is important to consult with a medical professional to understand the expected investment and plan accordingly.

What is the success rate of hair transplantation?

The percentage of transplanted hair that grows back is used to determine the success of a hair transplant. If the percentage reaches 80% or greater, the transplant is considered successful. On average, 95% to 98% of hair transplants are successful, particularly when the Follicular Unit Extraction (FUE) method is employed.
