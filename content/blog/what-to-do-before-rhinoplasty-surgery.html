What is Rhinoplasty?

Rhinoplasty is a surgical procedure used in aesthetics and health. Rhinoplasty is often applied when the shape of the nose is not aesthetically pleasing.

In Which Cases Is Rhinoplasty Performed?

Rhinoplasty, commonly referred to as a nose job, is performed for a variety of reasons, both cosmetic and functional. Here are the main cases in which rhinoplasty is typically performed: Cosmetic Reasons Aesthetic Enhancement:
    • Shape and Size Adjustment: To change the size, shape, or proportions of the nose.
    • Nasal Tip Refinement: To reshape the nasal tip if it is bulbous, drooping, or upturned.
    • Bridge Modification: To remove a hump or correct a depression on the nasal bridge.
    • Nostril Size and Shape: To adjust the size and shape of the nostrils if they are too wide, large, or asymmetrical.
    • Symmetry Improvement: To enhance facial symmetry by aligning the nose with other facial features.
Functional Reasons Medical and Health Issues:
    • Breathing Difficulties: To correct structural issues that impair breathing, such as a deviated septum.
    • Nasal Obstruction: To address nasal obstructions caused by enlarged turbinates or nasal polyps.
    • Sinus Problems: To alleviate chronic sinusitis or recurring sinus infections that are due to structural nasal issues.
Trauma and Reconstruction Post-Traumatic Reconstruction:
    • Injury Repair: To correct deformities and restore the appearance and function of the nose after an injury or accident.
    • Fracture Correction: To realign and reshape nasal bones that have been fractured.
Congenital Defects Birth Defects:
    • Congenital Abnormalities: To correct congenital nasal deformities or defects such as a cleft lip and palate.
Revision Surgery Secondary Rhinoplasty:
    • Revision of Previous Surgery: To correct or improve the results of a previous rhinoplasty that did not meet the patient's expectations or caused new issues.
Combined Reasons Cosmetic and Functional:
    • Many rhinoplasty procedures simultaneously address aesthetic and functional concerns, such as improving the nose's appearance while correcting a deviated septum to enhance breathing.
Psychological and Social Reasons Self-Confidence and Social Perception:
    • Enhancing Self-Esteem: To improve self-esteem and confidence by altering a feature the patient feels self-conscious about.
    • Social and Professional Factors: Sometimes, individuals seek rhinoplasty to align their appearance with societal or professional standards.

What Should be the Expectations of Patients Before Rhinoplasty Surgery?

Before undergoing rhinoplasty surgery, patients should have realistic expectations and a thorough understanding of the procedure. Here are some key points they should consider: Consultation and Planning Initial Consultation:
    • Discuss your goals and reasons for wanting the surgery.
    • Review your medical history and undergo a physical examination.
    • Understand the surgeon's experience and view before-and-after photos of previous patients.
Discussion of Options:
    • Learn about the different techniques and approaches.
    • Understand the limitations and what can realistically be achieved.
Pre-Surgery Expectations Preparation:
    • Follow pre-surgery instructions given by the surgeon, such as avoiding certain medications, quitting smoking, and fasting.
    • Arrange for someone to drive you home and assist you post-surgery.
Health Considerations:
    • Ensure you are in good health to minimize risks.
    • Discuss any allergies or pre-existing conditions with your surgeon.
Understanding the Procedure Surgical Details:
    • Understand the type of anesthesia that will be used.
    • Learn about the surgical steps and duration of the procedure.
    • Be aware of where the surgery will take place (hospital or clinic).
Recovery and Post-Surgery Care Recovery Time:
    • Plan for time off work and social activities.
    • Understand that swelling and bruising are normal and may take weeks to months to fully resolve.
Post-Surgery Instructions:
    • Follow the surgeon’s post-operative care instructions carefully.
    • Attend all follow-up appointments to monitor healing.
Risks and Complications Potential Risks:
    • Be aware of possible complications such as infection, bleeding, or adverse reactions to anesthesia.
    • Understand that there may be a need for revision surgery in some cases.
Emotional and Psychological Preparation Realistic Expectations:
    • Have a clear and realistic idea of the outcomes.
    • Understand that results may not be fully visible for several months.
Emotional Readiness:
    • Ensure you are emotionally prepared for the changes in your appearance.
    • Seek support from friends, family, or a counselor if needed.
Financial Considerations Cost:
    • Be clear on the total cost of the surgery, including surgeon’s fees, anesthesia, and facility costs.
    • Understand the payment options and whether any part of the procedure is covered by insurance.
By thoroughly preparing and having clear expectations, patients can ensure they are making informed decisions and are ready for the changes and recovery process associated with rhinoplasty surgery.

What to do Before Rhinoplasty Surgery?

Preparing for rhinoplasty surgery involves several important steps to ensure the best possible outcome and minimize risks. Here’s a comprehensive list of what to do before undergoing rhinoplasty: Medical Preparation Consultation with a Surgeon:
    • Discuss Goals: Clearly communicate your reasons for wanting the surgery and your desired outcome.
    • Medical History: Provide a complete medical history, including any previous surgeries, allergies, and current medications.
    • Physical Examination: Undergo a thorough examination of your nose and facial structure.
    • Photographs: Expect to have photos taken for pre- and post-surgery comparison.
Medical Tests:
    • Blood Tests: Complete any required blood tests to ensure you are in good health for surgery.
    • Other Tests: Depending on your health and age, you might need additional tests, such as an EKG or chest X-ray.
Medication Review:
    • Current Medications: Discuss your current medications with your surgeon. You may need to stop taking certain medications that can increase bleeding, such as aspirin, ibuprofen, and other non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).
    • Supplements: Avoid herbal supplements and vitamins that can affect blood clotting, such as vitamin E, ginkgo biloba, and fish oil.
Lifestyle and Habits Smoking and Alcohol:
    • Quit Smoking: Stop smoking at least two weeks before and after surgery to promote better healing and reduce the risk of complications.
    • Avoid Alcohol: Refrain from consuming alcohol for at least 48 hours before the surgery.
Healthy Diet:
    • Balanced Diet: Maintain a healthy and balanced diet to boost your immune system and overall health.
    • Hydration: Stay well-hydrated in the days leading up to surgery.
Pre-Surgery Logistics Transportation and Support:
    • Arrange Transportation: Plan for someone to drive you to and from the surgery, as you will not be able to drive yourself.
    • Post-Surgery Care: Arrange for someone to stay with you for at least the first 24 hours after surgery to assist with your needs.
Recovery Space:
    • Prepare a Comfortable Area: Set up a comfortable recovery area at home with essentials such as pillows, medications, ice packs, and entertainment.
    • Stock Up: Have easy-to-prepare meals and snacks available for the first few days post-surgery.
Day Before Surgery Fasting and Medication:
    • Follow Fasting Instructions: Follow your surgeon’s instructions regarding fasting before surgery, typically no food or drink after midnight.
    • Take Prescribed Medications: Take any medications your surgeon has instructed you to take on the morning of surgery with a small sip of water.
Hygiene:
    • Shower: Take a shower and wash your face with antibacterial soap to reduce the risk of infection.
    • Avoid Makeup and Products: Do not apply any makeup, lotions, or facial products on the day of surgery.

Is There a List of Products That Should Not Be Used Before Surgery?

Do not use the following products 1 month before your surgery date: fish oil, flaxseed, products containing omega 3, ginseng, garlic, ginger, or vitamin E. There is no problem with other vitamins (A, B, C, D). Apart from that, mixed herbal teas, legumes, broccoli, milk etc. Gas-producing foods such as gas should not be consumed excessively. To prevent and reduce oedema and swelling, it is necessary to stay away from mineral water with high sodium and foods with high salt content such as pickles. Avoid blue, red, and purple vegetables and fruits.

Are Blood Tests Done Before Surgery?

You come for surgery 3 days before your surgery. EKG, chest X-ray, and blood tests are performed, which can also show bleeding disorders. If you have had an operation before and your bleeding is more than normal, or if your bleeding lasts longer than normal when you have a cut, you should warn your doctor about this.

Can I Have Nose Surgery During My Period?

Bleeding, swelling, and bruising are more common during menstruation, as the narrowing properties of nasal capillaries are reduced due to oedema. In addition, women are more sensitive and stressed during this period.
