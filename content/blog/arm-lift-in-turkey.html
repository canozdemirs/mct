What are the risks of arm lifting?

Arm lift dangers include: 
  • Severe or abnormal scarring
  • Excess amount of bleeding
  • Damage to muscles, blood vessels, or nerves,
  • Numbness
  • Infection at the surgical site
  • Separation of wounds (dehiscence)
  • Fluid encumbrance (seroma): An accumulation of clear fluid in a tissue, organ, or body cavity that results in a lump or mass. Although it normally goes away on its own, a needle may be needed to drain it.

What is the procedure of arm lift operation?

Usually, a brachioplasty surgery lasts between one and two hours.
  • Initially, you are given medicine to help you feel comfortable. General anesthesia, which renders you unconscious and blocks sensation, may be included in this. You might get a local anesthetic, which calms you down and numbs the operative region.
  • On the inside or back of your arm, the surgeon makes an incision. The extent of the surgery determines the size of the incision.
  • Your doctor trims away extra skin, and he or she may also use liposuction to remove extra fat. To tighten the supporting tissue, they employ stitches (sutures).
  • Sutures are used to close the incision before the surgery is finished. To lower the chance of infection, they bandage your arm with sterile gauze as well.

What to expect after surgery in Turkey?

After brachioplasty (arm lift) surgery in Turkey, it is normal to experience some swelling and bruising. Most patients begin to feel better shortly after the operation, although mild discomfort may persist for one to two weeks and gradually subside as recovery progresses.

Your surgeon will provide detailed aftercare instructions, including the use of a compression garment to help reduce swelling, support healing, and speed up recovery. During the healing period, it is important to avoid strenuous activities, particularly those involving heavy lifting or excessive arm movement.

There will be a scar along the incision line; however, experienced surgeons in Turkey take great care to place incisions in discreet areas and minimize their visibility as much as possible. The typical recovery period is around two weeks, after which most patients can return to daily activities.

To reduce the risk of infection, bandages will be applied to the incision site. These bandages should be changed regularly according to your doctor’s instructions. Your surgeon will explain how often bandage changes are needed and how to properly care for your wound to ensure a smooth and safe recovery.

What to pay attention to after surgery?

It's critical to take good care of your incisions right away after your arm lift in order to minimize the visibility of your scars. First and foremost, it's imperative that you keep your incisions dry and clean. This will lessen the chance of infection, sluggish healing, and severe scarring. Second, wait until your incisions are completely healed before touching them directly. Third, make sure to thoroughly wash your hands with antibacterial soap before changing your bandages and dressings. Finally, follow your surgeon's instructions for taking all antibiotics exactly. If you have aesthetic concerns, in order to reduce the visibility of scars:
  • For the first two weeks following surgery, sleep with your arms up to lessen swelling.
  • Try not to get exposed to UV rays. Overexposure to the sun can lead to hyperpigmentation, which will make your scars stand out more. Therefore, it is recommended to apply high-SPF sunscreen for at least the first two months following surgery.
  • Utilize a silicone-based scar cream of the highest quality.
  • Massage the scars. Gently massaging your arm lift scars can help relieve any discomfort. The thickened scar tissue will be gradually broken up during this procedure, which will also make the incision site look better.
  • Avoid using tobacco or other products containing nicotine. 
  • Try applying concealer. You can wear concealer makeup while your scars are still visible. Only totally healed incisions should be treated with this. Patients can try a pressed powder concealer, which is less likely to come off than a liquid one, to allay worries about makeup transferring to clothing.
