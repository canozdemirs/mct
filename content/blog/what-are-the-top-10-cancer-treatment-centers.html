What Are The Types Of Cancer Treatments?

Surgery For Cancer

The first aim of cancer surgery is to remove the cancer completely from your body and provide its treatment. The surgeon usually does this by cutting out the cancerous tissue along with some surrounding healthy tissue to make sure all the cancer is removed. The surgeon may also remove several lymph nodes to decide whether the cancer has spread. This way, your doctor can evaluate your chances of treatment and whether you need further treatment. In cases of breast cancer, your doctor may remove all of the breast tissue (mastectomy) or remove only the portion containing the cancerous tissue and surrounding tissue (lumpectomy) to remove the cancer. In cases of lung cancer, your doctor may remove part of the lung (lobectomy) or an entire lung (pneumonectomy) during the procedure to make sure the cancer is completely removed. In both of these instances, the surgeon may also remove a few lymph nodes in the operating area during the operation so that they can see if the cancer has spread.

Radiation Therapy

Radiation therapy is a type of cancer treatment that uses intense energy beams to kill cancer cells. Radiation therapy mostly uses X-rays, but protons or other types of energy can also be used. Radiation therapy focuses on cells, destroying the genetic material that controls how cells grow and divide. While radiation therapy damages both healthy and cancerous cells, the goal of radiation therapy is to destroy as few normal, healthy cells as possible. Normal cells can usually repair most of the damage caused by radiation. More than half of all cancer patients receive radiation therapy as part of their cancer treatment. Doctors use radiation therapy to treat almost any type of cancer. Radiation therapy is also useful in the treatment of some noncancerous (benign) tumors.

Chemotherapy

Chemotherapy, which literally means "treatment with drugs", is the term used for treatment using cancer drugs (also called anti-cancer drugs) that mostly affect cancer cells. Chemotherapy can be used alone in the treatment of cancer, or it can be used to create a complementary effect to other treatment methods. Depending on the type of cancer, chemotherapy may have different purposes;to treat cancer, controlling cancer (preventing the spread of cancer and slowing its growth), and eliminating symptoms caused by cancer (eliminating or relieving pain and similar symptoms).

Immunotherapy

Cancer cells, which are composed of the body's own cells, have a different structure from healthy cells. That's why the body's defense system fights cancer cells. However, cancer cells can make some differentiations and prevent the immune system from recognizing them and taking action. The ability of cancer cells to hide themselves is due to the proteins they carry. In addition to all these, cancer cells can mutate. This makes it easier for cancer cells detected by the immune system to change and hide from the defense system. As a result, the body's own immune system's ability to destroy cancer cells is reduced or completely lost. Immunotherapy treatment is a form of biological treatment. In other words, the drug given to the body during immunotherapy ensures that the person's own immune system becomes active. The activated immune system recognizes cancer cells and destroys them.

Targeted Therapy

Targeted cancer therapies are drugs designed to interfere with specific molecules required for tumor growth and progression.Conventional cytotoxic (cell-killing) chemotherapies kill rapidly dividing cells in the body, usually by inhibiting cell division. The main goal of targeted Therapy is to deliver treatments to cancer cells that are more sensitive and potentially less harmful.

Hormone therapy

Hormone therapy is a cancer treatment that uses hormones to slow or stop the growth of the cancer. This method of treatment is used to treat certain cancers that use hormones to grow, such as prostate cancer and breast cancer.

Stem Cell Transplant

Stem cell transplants are mostly used in the treatment of cancers originating from blood and bone marrow. In stem cell transplantation, the patient's bone marrow is first emptied by administering high-dose chemotherapy. Then, stem cells collected from a donor with a compatible tissue type are infused into the patient. These stem cells settle in the recipient’s bone marrow, begin to multiply, and gradually restore healthy blood cell production, improving the patient’s underlying blood disease.

The cost of stem cell transplantation can vary depending on several factors, including the type of transplant (autologous or allogeneic), the patient’s medical condition, the need for a compatible donor, the length of hospitalization, and post-transplant follow-up care. Additional expenses may include pre-transplant evaluations, chemotherapy protocols, laboratory tests, and supportive treatments.
