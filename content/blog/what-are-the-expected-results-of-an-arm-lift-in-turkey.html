How does the recovery process affect the appearance of the arms in the first few weeks?

After arm lift surgery, the first few weeks of recovery play a significant role in the appearance and feel of the arms. Patients typically notice significant changes immediately after surgery, but it's normal for the arms to appear swollen, bruised, or slightly uneven in the early stages. Swelling is the most common effect during the first week. The body responds to the surgery by retaining fluid, which can cause the arms to appear more swollen than expected. This is temporary and gradually decreases over the next two to four weeks. Bruising may also occur, contributing to this temporary uneven appearance, but it usually disappears within two weeks. During this initial period, the arms may feel tight or stiff due to skin repositioning and internal healing. Some discomfort or mild pain is expected, and gentle movements while avoiding strenuous activities are recommended to prevent stiffness. Compression garments are often recommended to support the arms, reduce swelling, and help the skin adjust to its new shape. By the end of the second to fourth week, swelling begins to subside, and the arms begin to have smoother contours. Patients usually notice a gradual improvement in firmness and shape as the tissues settle. However, the final results are not yet visible, and subtle changes will continue over the next few months as healing progresses.
