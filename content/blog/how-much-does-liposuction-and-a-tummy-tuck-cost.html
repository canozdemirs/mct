What is liposuction?

Liposuction is a cosmetic procedure aiming to remove unwanted fat from the body. Liposuction involves the suction of fat that is hard to eliminate by exercise and diet. It can be done to areas such as the stomach, hips, thighs, buttocks, arms, or neck. Besides removing excess fat, it also shapes the area, which is known as contouring. Liposuction is preferred by patients with average weight and in areas of tight skin. Besides cosmetic purposes, liposuction can also be used for health conditions such as lymphoedema, a long-term swelling of arms and legs, and lipoedema, in which fat builds up abnormally in legs, buttocks, and thighs.

Why is liposuction done?

As mentioned above, liposuction is used to remove excess fat from a specific area, such as the abdomen, upper arms, buttocks, calves, chest, hips, thighs, chin, and neck. Other than this purpose, liposuction can also be used in gynecomastia, in which men develop extra breast tissue. Overall, liposuction reduces the number and volume of fat cells. Results tend to be permanent if the weight is kept the same. However, liposuction is not done to help with dimpled skin from cellulite and remove stretch marks.

What are the risks of liposuction?

Like any other surgery, liposuction carries certain risks, such as bleeding and reaction to anesthesia. There are also other risks specific to liposuction:
  • The skin may feel bumpy or wavy if the fat is removed unequally.
  • Pockets of fluid may form under the skin, requiring draining with a needle.
  • Treated areas may experience numbness which can be temporary or permanent.
  • An internal organ may be punctured with the tube used during the surgery.

How to prepare for liposuction?

A qualified surgeon is needed for liposuction. After your doctor’s examination, a treatment plan will be determined. You need to tell every detail about your medical history for the sake of the surgery. Certain drugs, such as blood thinners or nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, will be recommended to stop being used a certain time before the surgery. According to the amount of fat that will be removed, the patient may leave right after the surgery or stay at the hospital for a day or two. For both cases, finding someone to drive you home after the surgery will be useful.

What happens before liposuction?

Before the surgery, some marks and lines will be drawn on your body to ensure the specific area that will be treated. Also, some photos before the surgery may be taken for comparison after the surgery. The liposuction technique will be determined according to the area of treatment and whether the patient had liposuction before. These techniques include:

Suction-assisted liposuction

It is the most common technique in liposuction. Medicines mixed with salt water are injected into the specific area. Salt water is used because it helps with the fat removal process, and medicines are for pain relief and blood vessel narrowing. After the injection, a small incision will be made to insert a thin tube under the skin. This tube is connected to a vacuum that will suck out the fat and fluid from the body.

Ultrasound-assisted liposuction

UAL is used in combination with traditional liposuction. A metal rod is inserted under the skin, which will put a certain kind of energy leading to the opening of the fat cell walls. This eventually will break down the fat so that fat can be removed easily.

Laser-assisted liposuction

A laser fiber is put into the skin using a small incision. This fiber will break down fat deposits. Then a thin tube is used to remove the fat.

What happens during liposuction?

Liposuction can be carried out under local or general anesthetics. A healthcare team will be monitoring the heart rate, blood pressure, and blood oxygen level of the patient during the surgery.

What to expect after liposuction?

If the patient is given general anesthesia, they will wake up in a recovery room. They may experience some pain, swelling, and bruising after the surgery. They may also be given some medications for pain relief. Temporary drains may be left in the body after the surgery to help drain the fluid. In addition, wearing tight garments may be recommended by the doctor to reduce the swelling.

How does recovery happen after liposuction?

Although it depends on the procedure and the volume of the area that was treated, full recovery can be seen after the swelling is gone, which can take up to six months. Generally, stitches will be removed after a week of surgery, and contact sports can be resumed after four to six weeks.

What are the possible side effects of liposuction?

Below you can find some side effects that can be seen after liposuction:
  • Bruising
  • Swelling
  • Numbness
  • Scars
  • Inflammation
  • Leaking fluid from the cuts
  • Swollen ankles if the ankles were treated.

What is a tummy tuck?

A tummy tuck is also known as abdominoplasty. It is also a cosmetic procedure that aims to interfere with the shape and appearance of the abdomen. A tummy tuck also involves the removal of excess fat, just like in liposuction, but it is specific to the abdomen. It will tighten abdominal muscles and flatten the abdomen.  

Why is a tummy tuck done?

A tummy tuck can be done to remove loose and excess skin and fat. It can also be done to remove stretch marks. Who can get a tummy tuck?

Both men and women can go through a tummy tuck. Patient’s general health should be in good condition, and their weight should be stable. It is better if the patient is not smoking. People with excess fat, poor elasticity of the skin, or weakened connective tissue in the abdomen due to frequent weight changes, pregnancy, c-sections, aging, and abdominal surgery can have a tummy tuck.

When to avoid tummy tuck?

A tummy tuck should be avoided or postponed if you are in one of the below conditions:
  • If you are planning on having children. This is because, during pregnancy, vertical muscles that were tightened with tummy tuck will be separated again.
  • If you are considering losing a significant amount of weight. It would be best if you had a stable weight for tummy tuck surgery.
  • If you have a severe chronic condition such as heart disease or diabetes
  • If you have a body mass index that is higher than 30
  • If you are a smoker
  • If you had abdominal surgery before, that led to significant scar tissue forming.

What are the types of tummy tuck?

There are three types of tummy tuck which are:

Complete abdominoplasty

It is preferred for patients requiring the most correction. The skin and muscle will be reshaped, and an incision will be made at the bikini line. The length of the incision will be determined based on the amount of extra skin. To free the belly button from the surrounding tissue, an incision around the belly button will also be made.

Partial or mini-abdominoplasty

This procedure requires smaller incisions and is mostly preferred by patients who have less excess skin. The belly button will not be touched most of the time.

Circumferential abdominoplasty

This surgery happens in the back area of the body. Liposuction or circumferential abdominoplasty can be done if a patient has excess fat in the back and in the abdomen. The difference between the two procedures is that circumferential abdominoplasty removes both skin and fat from the hip and back areas. After all the procedures, there will be stitches and bandages at the incision sites.

What are the risks of a tummy tuck?

Below you can find some risks of tummy tuck surgery:
  • A seroma, which is fluid accumulation, can be seen. Drain tubes are left at the incision sites after the surgery to avoid seroma to reduce the excess fluid.
  • The incision line may heal poorly, or it may start to separate. To prevent infections, your doctor may prescribe you some antibiotics.
  • Fatty tissue deep under the skin in the abdomen may be damaged during the surgery. Tissue damage risk is increased in patients who smoke. Tissue may or may not heal depending on the size of the area.
  • Nerves in the abdomen may be affected during the surgery. This may lead to changes in skin sensation. These changes may involve a reduction in sensation or numbness. Changes in skin sensation tend to diminish after a certain amount of time.

How to prepare for a tummy tuck?

Before a tummy tuck, the doctor will review your medical history. It would help if you answered all of the doctor’s questions correctly to avoid any complications during the surgery. Make sure to mention your allergies if you have any. In order to determine the right treatment plan, you will go through a physical exam. To have a successful surgery,
  • Avoid smoking before surgery because it reduces blood flow and slows down the healing process. Smoking also can lead to tissue damage, which was mentioned before. Any form of nicotine, such as gums, patches, and e-cigarettes, should also be cut off a month before the surgery.
  • Try to eat well-balanced, as it is important for the healing process.
  • Stop taking aspirin, anti-inflammatory drugs, and herbal supplements before the surgery, as they can increase bleeding.
  • Try maintaining a stable weight for at least twelve months before the tummy tuck surgery.
  • Try to set up someone to drive you home after your hospital stay, as you will probably be in pain.

What happens before and during tummy tuck?

A tummy tuck will be performed in a hospital under general anesthesia. Some incisions will be made during the tummy tuck to remove excess skin and fat between the belly button and the pubic hair. Connective tissue is also tightened that is located above the abdominal muscles. Incisions above the pubic hair will be stitched, and this stitching will leave a scar that will not be seen due to its location. Some antibiotics may be given to the patient during the surgery to prevent infection. A tummy tuck will usually be done in about two to three hours.

What happens after a tummy tuck?

You may be left with small tubes at the incision sites after the surgery to help with fluid drainage. Your healthcare team will help with walking right after the surgery to prevent blood clot formation. In addition, you will be prescribed pain medication. You will also be informed about how to empty and care for your drainage tubes, as they can be left in place for several days. You will be recommended to wear an abdominal garment that will support your abdomen and prevent fluid accumulation. Please be careful when moving within the first six weeks. Also, avoid positions that will strain your incision line so that the wound will not be opened.
