What is Ear Correction (Otoplasty)?

Otoplasty or ear surgery is plastic surgery to correct aesthetic or functional problems in the ear area. This procedure can reposition and reshape the ear and correct any structural problems or deformities associated with the injury. Patient Profile Otoplasty is a procedure of great interest for both cosmetic and reconstructive purposes. In the first, patients seek to correct aesthetic problems such as protruding, deformed, deformed, or too large ears, while reconstructive surgery is performed to correct structural problems that were present at birth or caused by injury. For ear correction surgery in Turkey, you must be healthy and ideally a non-smoker. Otoplasties can be performed safely in healthy children and are most often performed on children starting at an age when their ear cartilage is sufficiently developed, usually as early as five years of age.

Otoplasty Procedure

Otoplasty usually takes two to three hours and is performed under general, local, or intravenous sedation. In most cases, the incision is made behind the ear or in front, within the natural contour of the ear, to make the incision less visible during the healing process. Cartilage is formed and sutures are needed to close the incision.

Types of Otoplasty

1- Incisionless Otoplasty:

One of the otoplasty procedures used to treat protruding ears is cut-out otoplasty. While other surgical treatments can be used to correct the condition, otoplasty without incisors is a minimally invasive surgery with a shorter recovery time and reduced risk of problems.

2- Lop-Ear Otoplasty:

Lop-ear otoplasty is a surgical treatment to treat ear deformities, also known as ear deformities, in which the top of the ear bends forward. The severity of these malformations varies from mild to severe. Folding the top of the ear can hide the natural details of the ear, make the ear look very small, and make the ear "pop" more than usual. Currently, clinics in Turkey offer you the best chance of getting it removed.

3- One Ear Otoplasty:

The otoplasty technique should only be operated on one patient's ear. As usual with other otoplasty techniques, the main goal is to correct imperfections or improve appearance. Risks and Side Effects

Like any surgery, otoplasty can be risky. Here are the potential risks:
  • Scars: Although scars are lifelong, they are closed behind or between the folds in your ear.
  • Asymmetrical ear position: This can lead to the development of changes that occur during the recovery period. In addition, surgery may not be able to correct an existing imbalance.
  • Changes in Tissue Sensitivity: Moving your ear during an otoplasty can temporarily change how your skin feels. Change is rarely irreversible.
  • Occurrence of an allergic reaction: Possible allergic reaction to medical tape or other objects used during or after treatment.
