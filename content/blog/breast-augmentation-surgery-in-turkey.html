What are the breast implant-associated anaplastic large cell lymphoma?

A relationship between breast implants and the development of anaplastic large cell lymphoma (ALCL), a rare immune system cancer, has been found by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA). This condition has been termed breast implant-associated anaplastic large cell lymphoma (BIA-ALCL). The FDA thinks that women with breast implants with textured surfaces are very low in BIA-ALCL but BIA-ALCL risk is increased. However, this does not indicate that these implants cause BIA-ALCL. More research is needed to understand the relationship between the condition and breast implants.

Breast augmentation surgery can be performed in a surgical center or in a hospital's outpatient facility. The procedure rarely requires a hospital stay after the surgery, you can go home if you want. During the breast augmentation surgery:

Your plastic surgeon will make a single incision in one of the areas such as wrinkle under your breast (under the breast), under your arm (armpit) or around your nipple (periareolar) to insert the breast implant. After your surgeon completes this procedure, he or she will separate your breast tissue from the muscles and connective tissue of your breast. This procedure is done to create a pocket behind or in front of the outermost muscle of the chest wall (pectoral muscle). Your surgeon inserts the implant into this pocket and centers it behind your nipple. Saline implants are placed empty and silicone implants are pre-filled with silicone gel. It is then placed in place and filled with sterile saline. After the implant is placed, the surgeon's cut is closed with stitches and bandaged with skin glue and surgical tape.

After the breast augmentation surgery:

There may be pain, swelling, and bruising for a few weeks after breast augmentation surgery. In addition, after breast augmentation, scars heal over time, but the scars do not disappear completely. It may be helpful to wear a compression bandage or sports bra for extra support and positioning of breast implants during your healing process. Your surgeon will prescribe the most appropriate pain reliever for your pain. You should follow your surgeon's instructions to return to regular activities, on the other hand, if you do not have a physically demanding job, it is possible to return to work within a few weeks. You should avoid strenuous activities for at least two weeks after surgery, such as anything that could increase your heart rate or blood pressure. Remember that your breasts will be sensitive to physical contact or jarring movements during your healing process. When your surgeon uses non-self-absorbent sutures in your breast augmentation surgery or places drainage tubes near your breasts, you will need to attend a follow-up appointment for removal. If you notice warmth and redness in your breast after breast augmentation surgery or if you have a fever, you may have an infection in such cases, you should contact your surgeon as soon as possible.
