Hair follicles are typically extracted from locations such as the neck where hair follicles are strong and transferred to areas where hair is thinning in this process, providing a permanent cure for hair loss. Who experience hair loss?

Everyone loses hair on a daily basis, averaging 50 to 150 individual hairs every day. Usually, the hair grows back. However, if the hair follicle is weakened, this re-growth does not occur. As a result of natural aging and changes in the male hormone androgen, all men experience some degree of gradual hair loss throughout their lives. Women may also experience hair loss.

What are the hair transplant techniques?

FUE (Follicular Unit Extraction) Technique

Because of its minimally invasive nature and ability to create natural-looking results, the FUE hair transplant technique is very popular among patients seeking hair transplantation in Turkey. A specialized instrument is used to harvest hair follicles from the donor site, which is usually near the back of the head. The recipient site is then carefully injected with these hair follicles so that they can grow and thrive there. The steps of the procedure are as follows,
  • The rear of the skull has been shaved.
  • Individual hairs are plucked one at a time (grafts), and the grafts are inserted into tiny wounds in the scalp.
  • You'll have a lot of little scars, but they won't be obvious.
Hair transplants typically take one day, but you should not be required to stay overnight. If a big area is being treated, two or more sessions spaced a few months apart may be required. You will be instructed on how to care for your graft by your surgeon. You should be given a spray to help with hair growth and recovery.

Advantages

One of the primary benefits of the FUE technique is that it does not require any incisions or sutures, which means that patients can expect minimal scarring and a speedier recovery period when compared to other procedures. Furthermore, the FUE technique can be utilized to create a natural-looking hairline and can be tailored to each patient's specific requirements. FUT (Follicular Unit Transplantation) Technique

The FUT technique is a highly efficient hair transplant surgery that is advised for individuals who have significant hair loss and require a greater number of hair follicles to be transplanted. In contrast to the FUE and DHI techniques, the FUT process involves the surgical removal of a slice of tissue containing hair follicles from the donor region, which is often located at the back of the head. After carefully separating the individual follicular units from the patch, they are implanted into the recipient region. Only the area where the skin is removed is trimmed, not the entire head. The stages are as follows,
  • A small strip of hair-bearing skin is removed from the back of your head and separated into pieces, each carrying 1 to 4 hairs (hair grafts).
  • The grafts are then put into tiny wounds in the scalp.
  • Stitches are used to seal the wound where the hairs are extracted.
  • The scar on the back of your head will be noticeable unless you have extremely short hair.

Advantages

While the FUT technique is more intrusive than other treatments, it can produce great results for people who have experienced considerable hair loss. The technique is very customizable and may be tailored to each patient's specific needs and goals. The FUT technique also allows for the transplantation of more hair follicles in a single visit, which is very beneficial for patients seeking a more comprehensive restoration.

DHI (Direct Hair Implantation) Technique

Another common hair transplant surgery available in Turkey is the DHI hair transplant technique, which offers patients a minimally invasive option for restoring hair loss. During the treatment, a Choi pen or a specialized tool is used to implant individual hair follicles directly into the recipient area, eliminating the need for incisions or sutures.

Advantages

This precise and flawless implantation procedure produces a more natural-looking hairline and fuller hair. It doesn’t require incisions or sutures. Furthermore, the DHI hair transplant technique provides a more tailored approach to hair transplantation, making it perfect for patients who have smaller regions of hair loss or who want to increase the density of their current hair. The DHI procedure, which employs advanced equipment and competent medical specialists, offers patients a safe and effective remedy for hair loss.

What is microblend grafting?

The top and front of your scalp are affected by male or female pattern baldness. Fortunately, your scalp's back and sides are genetically intended to grow for the rest of your life. These "horseshoe" regions on the back and sides serve as your donor area. Hair grafted from these locations (known as an autograft) to your balding spots will eventually begin to grow and continue to develop. The capacity of autografted hair from healthy donor sites on your scalp to take root and develop normally when transplanted into balding (recipient) parts of your scalp is referred to as donor dominance. The grafts can be "mini" (follicular groupings) or "micro" (follicular grafts). One to three individual hair follicles are contained in each micrograft. Four to six hairs are seen in follicular clusters. Ten to twenty follicles were seen in older "punch" transplants. The benefits of follicular grafts are that they generate delicate, fine hair that is more similar to your hair growth previous to balding. Because the hairline is a true transition zone from the forehead to denser hair further back, the most natural-looking hairline is created by using one to three hair grafts.

What is the recovery process?

Most people can return to work three days after receiving a hair transplant. For the first two weeks after your procedure, you must be extremely careful with your transplanted hair. The grafts will have the risk of falling. To prevent scarring, you may be advised to limit your exercise during the first month.
  • After 2 to 5 days, any bandages can normally be removed, but the grafts should not be touched.
  • On day 6, you should be able to gently wash your hair by hand.
  • Non-dissolvable stitches can normally be removed after 10 to 14 days.
  • After a few weeks, the transplanted hair will frequently fall out and then begin to grow back.
  • After 6 months, new hair should start to appear.
  • The full effects should be visible within 12 to 18 months.
Each operation is followed by a morning wash of the hair. For five to seven days, strenuous physical activity should be avoided. Medication is used to reduce edema that may occur. Some crusting or small scabs may appear for seven to ten days, along with itching in the treatment areas. Any numbness at the donor or recipient sites normally goes away within a few weeks to a few months. Follow-up appointments are planned at four to six-month intervals to monitor progress and evaluate results.

What are the possible risks of hair transplant?

Although numerous negative effects and difficulties may occur with hair transplantation, in well-programmed and well-planted patients, these are minor. The side effects include
  • Edema: Edema may form in the hair transplantation and donor area, as well as on the forehead, and may last 3-4 days. With gravity, forehead edema generates edema surrounding the eye, which can cause the eye to close for 1-2 days. Forehead edema is also produced by the volume of anesthetic medicines used in the transplantation area, as well as their effect on circulation in the forehead as a result of excessive hair transplantation in the transplantation area.
  • Infection: Because hair transplantation is performed under sterile conditions, the danger of infection is extremely low. Local infections are particularly common in donor lanes and hair transplantation sites. When the infection spreads, systemic antibiotics should be administered. Infections that begin in the hair follicles are common in the transplanting region.
  • Long-term crusting in the donor and hair transplant area: Crusting following hair transplantation is a risk for infection growth. Following planting, this crusting should be cleaned from the area using a special shampoo. Antibiotic creams should be applied three to four times per day.
  • Erythema: Erythema is skin redness induced by an injury or similar inflammatory disorder.
