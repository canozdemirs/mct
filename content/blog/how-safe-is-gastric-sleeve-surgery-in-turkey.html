Turkish hospitals follow strict safety protocols to ensure that gastric sleeve surgery is performed under optimal conditions. Most hospitals are JCI-accredited or nationally certified, meaning they comply with international healthcare standards. Key safety measures include:
  • Pre-operative testing, including blood work, imaging, and anesthetic evaluation

  • Sterile surgical environments and infection control protocols

  • Emergency preparedness in case of rare complications

Post-surgery, patients are closely monitored and guided by a team that includes dietitians, psychologists, and nurses. Are there any risks or complications after the surgery?

As with any major surgery, gastric sleeve surgery does carry risks, but serious complications are rare, especially in accredited Turkish hospitals. The most common minor side effects include:
  • Nausea or acid reflux
  • Fatigue during the first week
  • Temporary changes in digestion or appetite
More serious but less common risks may include:
  • Bleeding or infection
  • Leaks from the staple line (less than 1% risk in experienced hands)
  • Nutrient deficiencies if dietary guidelines are not followed
  • Gallstones due to rapid weight loss
To minimize these risks, Turkish hospitals:
  • Perform thorough pre-op evaluations
  • Use advanced laparoscopic equipment
  • Provide detailed recovery plans and vitamin guidance
Close monitoring in the days after surgery and scheduled follow-ups also help identify any issues early. Overall, when the procedure is performed by an experienced team in a modern facility, gastric sleeve surgery is safe and well-tolerated.

How long will I stay in the hospital after the procedure?

After gastric sleeve surgery in Turkey, patients typically stay in the hospital for 2 to 3 days. This short stay allows the medical team to monitor your recovery and manage any immediate side effects like nausea or discomfort. Here’s what the timeline usually looks like:
  • Day 1: Surgery is performed (usually laparoscopically under general anesthesia)
  • Day 2: You begin drinking clear fluids and walking around
  • Day 3: If you’re stable, eating liquids without issues, and pain is under control, you’re discharged
Before leaving, you’ll receive:
  • Pain medication and vitamins
  • A detailed diet plan for the next few weeks
  • Contact information for support after returning home
Some clinics also offer hotel stays with nurse visits after discharge, giving you extra comfort while you recover. This short and well-supported hospital stay is one of the reasons why Turkey is a trusted destination for bariatric surgery.
