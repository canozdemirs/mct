Who are not ideal candidates for liposuction?

A liposuction operation could go wrong for a variety of reasons or even endanger your general health. Liposuction may not be right for you if you:
  • Have a low weight.
  • A BMI (Body mass index) greater than 25.
  • Possess a serious, potentially fatal illness or a condition that can make recovery challenging.
  • Have irrational hopes for how the process will go.
  • Possess bad skin quality. Saggy, loose skin cannot be improved by liposuction alone. Your doctor might advise liposuction in addition to extra skin removal if your goal is to get rid of loose skin.

What are the risks of the surgery?

Liposuction, like any operation, has hazards. These dangers include hemorrhage and anesthetic reactions. If the surgeon performs many treatments simultaneously or works on greater body surfaces, the chance of complications increases. How these risks affect you should be discussed with the surgeon. Talk with your surgeon in advance of the procedure about what to anticipate from the surgery. Dangers associated with liposuction include:
  • Irregularities in the contour. Because of uneven fat removal, poor skin elasticity, and scarring, your skin may appear bumpy, wavy, or withered. These changes could be permanent.
 
  • The accumulation of fluid. Seromas are temporary pockets of fluid that can occur beneath the skin. A needle may be required to empty them.
  • Numbness. In the treated locations, you may have temporary or permanent numbness. Nerves in the area may also be inflamed.
  • Infections. Skin infections are uncommon, although they do occur. A serious skin infection could be fatal. Internal piercing. A tiny tube used during surgery may occasionally puncture an internal organ if it penetrates too deeply. This could necessitate immediate surgery to repair the organ.
  • Fat embolism. Fat fragments may break off and become lodged in a blood artery. They could then congregate in the lungs or go to the brain. A fat embolism is a life-threatening medical condition.
  • Problems with the kidneys and the heart. Fluid changes when big amounts of liposuction are performed. This can result in potentially fatal renal, heart, and lung disorders.
  • Lidocaine toxicity. A drug called lidocaine is used to treat pain. It is frequently injected along with fluids during liposuction. Despite the fact that lidocaine is often harmless, lidocaine toxicity can occasionally happen and result in major issues with the heart and central nervous system.
