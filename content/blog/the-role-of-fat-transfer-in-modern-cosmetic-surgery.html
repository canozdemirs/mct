What is Fat Transfer?

Fat transfer, also known as fat grafting or fat injection, is a cosmetic procedure where fat is harvested from one area of the body (such as the abdomen, thighs, or flanks) and transferred to another area that could benefit from the added volume, like the face, breasts, hands, or buttocks. The process involves three main steps: fat extraction through liposuction, processing and purifying the fat cells and then reinjecting them into the target area. Fat transfer is popular because it uses the patient's natural tissue, reducing the risk of allergic reactions or rejection, and provides a more natural look and feel compared to synthetic fillers. Additionally, it offers the dual benefit of body contouring at the extraction site and volume enhancement at the target site. The results of fat transfer can be long-lasting, though some of the injected fat may be reabsorbed by the body over time, potentially requiring follow-up treatments.

Which Body Parts Can I Have Fat Transferred to?

Fat transfer is a versatile cosmetic procedure that can enhance volume, improve body contours, and create a more youthful appearance in multiple areas. On the face, fat can be used to smooth fine lines, fill hollow under-eye areas, and add volume to the cheeks or lips, delivering a natural, refreshed look. Breast augmentation with fat transfer offers a subtle, implant-free increase in size for a soft, natural appearance. The buttocks are another popular target, especially in procedures like the Brazilian Butt Lift, where fat is strategically added to achieve a fuller, rounder shape. Fat transfer can also rejuvenate the hands, restoring lost volume and reducing visible signs of aging. By using the body’s own fat, this procedure provides safe, natural-looking enhancements across multiple areas, making it a highly adaptable option for aesthetic improvement.

What Can Fat Transfer Help with?

Fat transfer can help with a range of cosmetic and restorative goals by using a person’s own fat to enhance volume, improve contours, and rejuvenate specific areas of the body. It is commonly used to address signs of aging, such as sunken cheeks, thinning lips, or hollowed areas under the eyes, providing a youthful fullness to the face. In addition, fat transfer can help achieve natural-looking augmentation in areas like the breasts and buttocks, offering an alternative to implants for those looking for subtle enhancement. For people with scars or contour irregularities, fat transfer can smooth out unevenness, creating a more balanced appearance. The procedure also restores volume to areas affected by aging, such as the hands, making them appear fuller and less bony. Overall, fat transfer offers a minimally invasive way to enhance and rejuvenate the body using natural tissue.

Does Fat Transfer Really Work?

Yes, fat transfer is an effective procedure that can provide long-lasting and natural-looking results, especially when performed by an experienced surgeon. The procedure is generally safe and successful, using the patient’s own fat to enhance areas like the face, breasts, buttocks, or hands. Because it uses natural tissue, the body is less likely to reject it, reducing risks associated with synthetic implants or fillers. However, not all the transferred fat cells survive in the new location; typically, about 50–80% of the injected fat cells survive, and the rest are reabsorbed by the body over time. To compensate, surgeons may initially inject a bit more fat than desired, knowing some will naturally dissolve. Results can vary depending on factors like the individual’s body, the area treated, and post-procedure care, but with proper technique and care, fat transfer can deliver satisfying, lasting results for many people.

Do Fat Transfers Last?

Fat transfers can offer long-lasting results, but longevity depends on factors like the treated area, the patient’s lifestyle, and the body’s response to the procedure. During a fat transfer, only a portion of the injected fat cells will permanently survive in the new location, usually between 50–80%, while the rest are gradually absorbed by the body within the first few months. The fat successfully establishes a blood supply in the new area and tends to stay there long-term, making the results potentially permanent. However, these results may fluctuate with significant weight changes or aging, as the fat cells will respond to the body’s natural metabolic processes. Following post-procedure care instructions, maintaining a stable weight, and leading a healthy lifestyle can help maximize and extend the longevity of the transferred fat. Is Fat Transfer Safe?

Fat transfer is widely regarded as a safe cosmetic procedure, especially when performed by a skilled and experienced surgeon. Since the technique uses the patient’s own fat, the risk of allergic reactions or rejection is much lower compared to synthetic fillers or implants. However, like any surgical procedure, there are potential risks, including infection, bleeding, or uneven results if the transferred fat does not survive evenly. In rare cases, serious complications such as fat embolism—where fat enters the bloodstream and blocks a blood vessel—can occur, particularly if the injection is done too deeply or improperly, emphasizing the importance of selecting a qualified provider.

Most patients experience mild side effects like swelling or bruising, which typically resolve within a few weeks. With proper technique and adherence to post-procedure care, fat transfer provides safe and natural-looking enhancements, making it a popular choice for facial rejuvenation, breast augmentation, and body contouring.

What Happens Before Fat Transfer Surgery?

Before a fat transfer surgery, patients undergo consultation with their surgeon to discuss their goals, assess their overall health, and determine whether they are a good candidate for the procedure. The surgeon will examine areas with excess fat (like the abdomen, thighs, or flanks) to decide where fat can be safely harvested and identify the target areas for augmentation. Patients may also be asked to stop smoking and avoid certain medications, such as blood thinners, for a specified period before the surgery to reduce risks of bleeding and aid in healing. Pre-operative instructions typically include fasting guidelines and preparing for post-surgery recovery, such as arranging transportation and having a support person if needed. Additionally, the surgeon may take pre-surgery photos for reference and to help track progress. Overall, careful planning and adherence to instructions are essential for a safe procedure and optimal results.

What Happens During Fat Transfer Surgery?

During fat transfer surgery, the process involves three key steps: fat harvesting, purification, and reinjection. First, the surgeon extracts fat from areas with excess fat, like the abdomen, thighs, or flanks, using liposuction under local or general anesthesia. This involves making small incisions and inserting a thin tube (cannula) to gently remove the fat cells. Next, the harvested fat is processed to remove impurities, excess fluids, and damaged cells, leaving only purified, healthy fat for injection. Finally, the surgeon carefully injects the purified fat into the target areas—such as the face, breasts, buttocks, or hands—using multiple small injections to create smooth, natural contours. The surgeon may slightly overfill the area, as some fat will be reabsorbed by the body in the healing process. Throughout the procedure, precise techniques are used to ensure even distribution and natural-looking results.

What Happens After Fat Transfer Surgery?

After fat transfer surgery, patients typically experience some swelling, bruising, and discomfort in both the areas where fat was harvested and the target areas where it was injected. The surgeon usually provides specific aftercare instructions, which may include wearing compression garments on the donor areas to minimize swelling and support healing. Patients are advised to avoid strenuous activities for a few weeks to allow the fat cells to settle and establish a blood supply in the new location. To help maximize fat survival, it’s important to avoid placing pressure on the treated areas, especially in cases like buttocks or facial fat transfers, as this can impact the results. The swelling usually subsides within a few weeks, and while initial results may be visible soon after, results take several months to fully stabilize. Following a healthy lifestyle and maintaining a stable weight can help ensure lasting results, as weight fluctuations can affect transferred fat.

What are the Risks of Fat Transfer?

Fat transfer is generally safe, but, like any surgical procedure, it comes with some risks. Common side effects include swelling, bruising, and mild discomfort, which usually resolve on their own within a few weeks. More serious risks, though rare, include infection at the injection or harvesting sites, blood clots, and fat necrosis (where some fat cells die, creating lumps under the skin). Another risk is fat reabsorption, where the body reabsorbs a portion of the transferred fat, potentially leading to less predictable or uneven results. In very rare cases, fat embolism may occur, where fat enters the bloodstream and blocks a blood vessel, posing serious health risks. These risks can be minimized by choosing an experienced, board-certified surgeon who uses safe techniques and by following post-procedure care instructions. Overall, fat transfer is considered low risk, especially when performed with proper planning and technique.
